# Ship video and GSAP, skip the runtimes

No connector, plugin or skill that can be downloaded will make TB Gym's homepage more premium: every design-generation server emits React + Tailwind or raw HTML, every animation editor's web runtime (Rive, Spline, LottieFiles' dotLottie player) runs on WebAssembly that the site's `script-src 'self'` policy blocks outright, and the pages the owner admires carry none of those runtimes. Of the five tools in the ChatGPT answer, two earn a place. **GSAP** is already installed, has been free for commercial use with every former Club plugin since April 2025, and can take SplitText (**3.6 KB gzipped**) and Flip (**9.7 KB**) without a CSP or hydration change ([GSAP Standard License](https://gsap.com/community/standard-license/), [unpkg gsap@3.15.0](https://unpkg.com/gsap@3.15.0/dist/)). **Jitter** is a browser tool that exports files rather than a runtime, so one month of Pro (needed for WebM and watermark-free output; the dollar price is JavaScript-rendered and unverified, about $16-19) produces the hero loop that plan row R6.2c waits for as MP4 + WebM + poster, which the current policy already allows ([Jitter export table](https://help.jitter.video/en/articles/5369843-export-your-work)). **Rive** is the strongest tool on the list and the wrong one for this page: 315-903 KB gzipped of WASM plus an app-wide `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` change, for a hero that is not interactive; it is a "later" for in-app success states only. **Spline** fails CSP, Lighthouse and the design brief at once, and **LottieFiles** is admissible only in its old `lottie-web` form (46 KB, no WASM) for small bespoke UI moments. The admired premium pages (Whoop, Oura, Eight Sleep, Tonal, Peloton, Future, Apple Fitness+) get their look from self-hosted video in modern codecs, owned typography and an asset pipeline, with GSAP on three of six and **zero Rive, three.js or 3D canvases**; the coaching competitors' tells are the visible marketing stack (Intercom bubbles, HubSpot forms, reCAPTCHA as the largest script on the page). The agent tooling worth installing is two free, no-sign-in items (the Angular team's CLI MCP and the Context7 plugin) plus GSAP's official skills, all at user scope so the unattended plan runner loads them; everything else is OAuth-gated, React-shaped or redundant with the Chrome DevTools, Playwright and axe stack already in the repo. Third-party services resolve the same way: a first-party "Talk to us" form, a WhatsApp link, proxied Plausible (1.4 KB, no cookies, no banner) and self-hosted media need no CSP change, while booking grids, chat bubbles, demo iframes, testimonial widgets and 3D viewers each cost a header change, a blank pre-render and 300-6,000 ms of phone main-thread time. The owner's willingness to pay buys the shoot, one month of Jitter Pro, $9 a month of Plausible and, later, a one-off font licence; it should not buy a connector. AI placeholder photographs for the partner meeting are best made in Adobe Firefly for one month (US$11.79 including VAT, Content Credentials embedded automatically), kept in a folder the production asset glob never copies, watermarked and tagged, refused by the build check `npm run build` already runs, and served only from a basic-auth host that sends `X-Robots-Tag: noindex`.

## Three constraints decide the five tools before taste does

The decision is mostly made by the nginx header before any tool is judged on looks. The policy actually shipped is stricter than the one quoted in the brief: `default-src 'self'; base-uri 'self'; form-action 'self'; frame-ancestors 'none'; object-src 'none'; img-src 'self' data:; style-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline'; script-src 'self'; connect-src 'self' ws: wss:` (`src/web/nginx.conf` line 35, mirrored in `src/web/scripts/serve-built.mjs` line 41). Because it declares no `media-src`, `font-src`, `frame-src` or `worker-src`, each of those falls back to `default-src 'self'`: same-origin video, fonts and workers are allowed, and every third-party iframe, script host and XHR origin is a header change (inference from the directive fallbacks). MDN states the rule that removes three of the five tools at once: "If a page has a CSP header and `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` isn't specified in the `script-src` directive, WebAssembly is blocked from loading and executing on the page" ([MDN script-src](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Reference/Headers/Content-Security-Policy/script-src)). Rive's web runtime fetches its WASM "from unpkg by default" ([Rive: preloading WASM](https://rive.app/docs/runtimes/web/preloading-wasm)); dotLottie's "~500 KB compressed" engine is "fetched from a CDN when the first player is constructed" ([dotlottie-web README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LottieFiles/dotlottie-web/main/packages/web/README.md)); Spline's runtime loads scenes from `prod.spline.design` and draws on a WebGPU canvas with a WebGL fallback ([@splinetool/runtime](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@splinetool/runtime)). The `connect-src 'self'` line blocks each of those fetches today, and self-hosting the files fixes only the fetch, not the WASM rule; inlining the bytes with `@rive-app/canvas-single` does not escape it either, because MDN's wording is about compiling WebAssembly at all (inference). No vendor page for Rive, LottieFiles or Spline states a CSP requirement; the `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` need is MDN's general rule applied to them, which is the first item that could not be verified against a vendor statement.

The second constraint is the pre-render. Angular's hydration guide requires that "the HTML produced by the server side rendering operation must not be altered between the server and the client", names "components that do direct DOM manipulation using native DOM APIs" as "the most common culprit", and tells integrators to run third-party DOM scripts in `afterNextRender` after hydration ([Angular hydration](https://angular.dev/guide/hydration)). `@defer` blocks render only their placeholder during the build-time render and "content visible on first load shouldn't be deferred, since that can raise cumulative layout shift" ([Angular @defer](https://angular.dev/guide/templates/defer)). Any canvas runtime therefore follows one pattern: the server renders a sized `<canvas>` or a poster `<img>` with explicit dimensions, and the runtime is created in the browser and draws into it (inference from the guide). The repo already enforces part of this: `npm run build` chains `ng build`, `scripts/inline-home-styles.mjs` and `npm run build:check`, and `scripts/check-prerender.mjs` fails the build when `index.html` holds an inline script ("which the CSP (script-src 'self') would refuse"), lacks the hydration mark, or still links the global stylesheet (`src/web/package.json`, `src/web/scripts/check-prerender.mjs`). A vendor embed that injects an inline config script, as Beamer's and Intercom's do, fails that check before it fails the CSP (inference from the check's regex). GSAP today sits inside both rules: `home-week-motion.ts` lazy-imports `gsap` and `gsap/ScrollTrigger` as one chunk and wraps every tween in `gsap.matchMedia()` under `WIDE_STORY = '(min-width: 1100px) and (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)'`, so the build-time render, phones and reduced-motion users get the static page (`src/web/src/app/features/home/home-week-motion.ts` lines 21-91).

The third constraint is arithmetic. Lighthouse weights Total Blocking Time at **30 %** and Largest Contentful Paint at **25 %** of the performance score, with 90-100 the green band ([Chrome: performance scoring](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/performance/performance-scoring)); its third-party audit flags any page where scripts from other domains block the main thread for **250 ms** or more during load ([Chrome: third-party summary](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/performance/third-party-summary)); the phone simulation runs at 1.6 Mbps with a 4x CPU slowdown, which the earlier visual research translated to roughly five seconds of throttled budget per megabyte (inference) ([Lighthouse throttling](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GoogleChrome/lighthouse/main/docs/throttling.md)). The homepage sits at 91 performance on a phone after R6.2b (project memory), so the headroom is a few hundred milliseconds of main-thread time and well under a megabyte of eager bytes, which is why a 315 KB WASM file or a 4,305 ms booking widget is not a design question. Two more audits shape the motion choices: web.dev's rule to "restrict animations to `opacity` and `transform`" so they stay on the compositor ([web.dev animations guide](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)), and Lighthouse's "Avoid non-composited animations" diagnostic, which notes that such animations "can look janky (not smooth) on low-end phones" and can raise CLS ([Chrome: non-composited animations](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/performance/non-composited-animations)).

## The admired pages ship video and GSAP, not Rive, Lottie or Spline

The research pass captured the owner's premium references on 2026-10-12 with a Playwright miner that counted every finished request and regex-scanned every JavaScript body for library signatures; the counts below come from those captures of the named sites. Five of the six brand homepages are Next.js builds on a headless CMS or transform CDN (Contentful, imgix, Cloudinary; Tonal is Shopify), all six self-host or license their brand fonts, and none embeds a YouTube, Vimeo or Wistia iframe. Across the six owner-named brands the tallies are GSAP 3 of 6, Lenis 1 of 6, Lottie 1 of 6 (plus Apple), Rive 0 of 6, three.js or any WebGL canvas 0 of 6, and pin-spacers 0 on every one. Lenis, Rive, three.js, SplitText and pin-spacers appear only on agency and award sites: landonorris.com runs 17 Rive objects driven by GSAP ScrollTrigger inside a 362 KB agency bundle ([Lando Norris](https://www.landonorris.com/)), 1Password's hero and logo wall are `.riv` files ([1Password](https://1password.com/)), Headspace's HTML references Lottie 18 times ([Headspace](https://www.headspace.com/)), and rive.app's own Framer-built homepage mixes 35 `.riv` references with 19 MP4 videos ([rive.app](https://rive.app/)). The coaching platforms separate from the premium set on a different axis than tracking. Zero of seven (Trainerize, TrueCoach, Everfit, Hevy Coach, PT Distinction, Mindbody, Glofox) ship GSAP, Lenis, Rive or three.js; the one Lottie is a WordPress plugin on TrueCoach next to `animate.css` from cdnjs, Splide and Swiper ([TrueCoach](https://truecoach.co/)). What they carry instead is visible marketing furniture: Intercom on Trainerize and TrueCoach, HubSpot forms and CTAs, Google reCAPTCHA as the single largest script on Trainerize's page at 347 KB, Vimeo iframes on Mindbody, Wistia on PT Distinction, Google Fonts links on three of them ([Trainerize](https://www.trainerize.com/), [Mindbody](https://www.mindbodyonline.com/), [PT Distinction](https://www.ptdistinction.com/)). Ad pixels, consent managers, session replay and Swiper carousels appear on both sides (Oura runs 21 ad hosts, Hevy Coach runs 3) and so do not separate them. A second scan with no consent given found that none of Whoop, Oura, Eight Sleep, Future or Trainerize enforces a strict `script-src`: Whoop's header allows `script-src * 'unsafe-inline' 'unsafe-eval'`, Oura and Future send no CSP, Eight Sleep and Trainerize send only `frame-ancestors` ([Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/), [Oura](https://ouraring.com/), [Eight Sleep](https://www.eightsleep.com/), [Future](https://www.future.co/), [Trainerize](https://www.trainerize.com/)). TB Gym's policy is stricter than all five, so no snippet of theirs can be copied as-is; the right reading is that the strict policy forces first-party patterns, not that the admired sites prove widgets are harmless (inference). The same scan found zero `<canvas>` elements, no Calendly or Cal.com embed, no Senja, Testimonial.to, Trustpilot or G2 widget host, and no chat launcher on Whoop, Oura or Future, which fixes the premium reading of "restraint": the brands show the product with their own media and nothing else in the viewport. The remaining gap between TB Gym and that set is media production, which no network layer can supply and which the shoot brief at `docs/design/homepage-shoot-brief.md` already covers; the earlier visual research reached the same conclusion from the screenshots, noting that Oura's 49 KB AVIF and Future's 64 KB WebP hero stills look no less premium than loops ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/), [Future](https://www.future.co/)).

## Jitter and GSAP cover the whole motion brief for one month of Pro

Jitter is "a fast and simple motion design tool on the web" whose output is files: GIF, MP4 and Lottie on Free, WebM and ProRes on Pro, transparent WebM, MOV and GIF only on Max and Ultra ([Jitter](https://jitter.video/), [Jitter export table](https://help.jitter.video/en/articles/5369843-export-your-work)). Free is capped at "720p / 30fps" with "Watermark on exports: Yes"; Pro lifts that to "1080p / 60fps" with no watermark and adds the WebM the hero needs; paid plans are "per editor / month billed annually" in USD "exclusive of VAT", and the dollar amounts are injected by JavaScript, so the pricing page read as "--" ([Jitter pricing](https://jitter.video/pricing)). "We don't offer trial plans" and "We don't offer a one-time payment option" ([Jitter: trial](https://help.jitter.video/en/articles/16761802-trial-jitter-for-free), [Jitter: compare plans](https://help.jitter.video/en/articles/14809073-compare-jitter-s-subscription-plans)); a third-party listing gives Pro at $19 a month ([Techjockey](https://www.techjockey.com/us/detail/jitter)) and the owner's own plan budgets "Jitter (about $16/month) for one month" (`docs/UI-REDESIGN-PLAN.md` line 2008), so the exact figure must be read in a browser before paying. Two cautions follow from the changelog: the Lottie export launched on 20 April 2022 "currently in beta", replaces text and mask animations with "a fade-in / fade-out", and was still receiving fidelity fixes on 30 June and 1 October 2026 ([Jitter Lottie export](https://jitter.video/changelog/2022-04-20-lottie-export/), [Jitter changelog](https://jitter.video/changelog/)); and there is no API, MCP server, CLI or desktop app anywhere in the 2025-2026 changelog, only "Jitter AI" inside the editor, so the agent's role is to consume the exported files ([Jitter changelog](https://jitter.video/changelog/)). What enters Angular is three static files under `public/` (the folder `angular.json` copies with its `**/*` glob, verified in `src/web/angular.json`) and a `<video poster autoplay muted loop playsinline>` in the server-rendered HTML, with the poster as the LCP element, `preload="none"` or `metadata`, no `loading="lazy"` on the hero video, a pause control because anything moving longer than five seconds needs one, and the poster alone under reduced motion, which is exactly the R6.2c row: "the owner makes the loop in Jitter (MP4 and WebM, with a poster frame); then a chat puts it in the hero, loaded late, with the poster for reduced motion" (`docs/UI-REDESIGN-PLAN.md` line 116) ([web.dev video performance](https://web.dev/learn/performance/video-performance), [DebugBear](https://debugbear.com/blog/optimize-video-lcp), [WCAG 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)). The peers that keep their scores hold loops at 9-15 s and 0.9-1.9 MB per breakpoint, and Unbounce's analysis of about 35,000 pages found hero video "neutral or even negative" for conversion, so the loop is a brand decision that must be measured against the still on a phone and kept switchable ([Peloton](https://www.onepeloton.com/), [Eight Sleep](https://www.eightsleep.com/), [Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/landing-page-optimization/video-on-landing-pages)).

GSAP's position changed in 2025 and the change is the whole reason it leads the list. "GSAP is 100% free for all users, thanks to Webflow's support"; the Standard License (effective 30 April 2025) says "all of GSAP including the plugins that were formerly 'members-only' like SplitText and MorphSVG" "can be used in commercial projects at no charge", the only prohibited use is building a no-code animation tool that competes with Webflow, and "AI-generated code is not a 'Prohibited Use'" ([GSAP pricing](https://gsap.com/pricing/), [GSAP Standard License](https://gsap.com/community/standard-license/)). The 3.13 release of 29 April 2025 rewrote SplitText "50% smaller" with masking wrappers and an `aria-label`/`aria-hidden` scheme ([GSAP 3.13](https://gsap.com/blog/3-13/)); 3.15.0 was published on 13 April 2026 ([npm gsap](https://registry.npmjs.org/gsap)). Measured from the 3.15.0 distribution, the core is **28.3 KB gzipped**, ScrollTrigger **18.0 KB** (both already loaded on wide screens), SplitText **3.6 KB**, Flip **9.7 KB**, ScrollSmoother 5.5 KB, MorphSVG 9.5 KB ([unpkg gsap@3.15.0](https://unpkg.com/gsap@3.15.0/dist/)). The docs themselves give the rules for the "three or four short scroll stages" the direction calls for: `pin` an element but "don't animate the pinned element itself", "only one pinned element is allowed" per trigger and "nested pinning is not supported", `anticipatePin: 1` avoids flashes on fast scrolls, `scrub` as a number adds seconds of smoothing, and `normalizeScroll` "forces scrolling to be done on the JavaScript thread", which is why it and ScrollSmoother stay off a phone-first page ([ScrollTrigger docs](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/)). SplitText should split after `document.fonts.ready`, use `mask: "lines"` and `autoSplit`, and keep `aria: "auto"` for plain headlines ([SplitText docs](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/SplitText/)); `gsap.matchMedia()` carries a `reduceMotion` condition and reverts every tween when a query stops matching, the mechanism `home-week-motion.ts` already uses ([gsap.matchMedia()](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/GSAP/gsap.matchMedia()/)). Flip animates from measured DOM state with transforms, which makes it the natural tool for stages whose composition changes between chapters (inference). For the agent, GSAP publishes `https://gsap.com/llms.txt` (an index to Markdown versions of every v3 docs page) and an official skills repository with eight skills including `gsap-scrolltrigger` and `gsap-performance`, installed with `npx skills add https://github.com/greensock/gsap-skills` or, in Claude Code, `/plugin marketplace add greensock/gsap-skills`; there is no GSAP MCP, and the community "GSAP MCP" servers in directories are unverified code ([gsap.com/llms.txt](https://gsap.com/llms.txt), [greensock/gsap-skills](https://github.com/greensock/gsap-skills)). Webflow's own homepage mentions `gsap` 120 times in its HTML, a reasonable proxy for what the maintainer ships on itself ([Webflow](https://www.webflow.com/)).

Rive is the best vector tool here and the costliest fit. Its pricing moved exports to paid plans on 20 October 2025: "Free exports play a Rive splash screen", Cadet at "$9/month annually" or "$17/month monthly" removes it, Voyager is $32 on the pricing page but $39 monthly or $304 a year in the docs (an unresolved conflict), Enterprise is $120 a seat, and "Runtimes remain open-source under MIT" with "No runtime fee" ([Rive pricing](https://rive.app/pricing), [Rive docs pricing](https://rive.app/docs/account-admin/pricing), [Rive $9 plan](https://rive.app/blog/rive-s-new-9-mo-plan)). The JavaScript is small (67.9 KB gzipped for `@rive-app/canvas`, 53.0 KB for `canvas-lite`, 63.8 KB for the recommended `webgl2`), but the WASM measured from unpkg is **314.7 KB gzipped for canvas-lite** (which drops text, audio, layouts and scripting), **849.0 KB for canvas** and **903.1 KB for webgl2**, and the single-file build is 1.25 MB ([bundlephobia @rive-app/canvas](https://bundlephobia.com/package/@rive-app/canvas), [unpkg canvas-lite rive.wasm](https://unpkg.com/@rive-app/canvas-lite/rive.wasm), [unpkg webgl2 rive.wasm](https://unpkg.com/@rive-app/webgl2/rive.wasm), [Rive: canvas vs WebGL](https://rive.app/docs/runtimes/web/canvas-vs-webgl)). There is no official Angular package and the community `ng-rive` was last published on 15 September 2023 for Angular 13-era peers ([npm ng-rive](https://registry.npmjs.org/ng-rive)); integration without a wrapper is straightforward (create the `Rive` instance on a pre-sized `<canvas>` in `afterNextRender`, call `cleanup()` on destroy, keep `autoplay: false` under reduced motion since the parameters page never mentions `prefers-reduced-motion`) ([Rive parameters](https://rive.app/docs/runtimes/web/rive-parameters)). Its agent story is real: an MCP "available only in the desktop Editor for Windows and macOS" (`claude mcp add --transport http rive http://127.0.0.1:9791/mcp`, with the Early Access app open) that can build artboards, state machines, data binding and scripts, and a CLI with a text format, RML, that is free during its preview and sets up `AGENTS.md`/`CLAUDE.md` for coding agents ([Rive MCP](https://rive.app/docs/editor/ai/mcp), [Rive CLI](https://rive.app/docs/cli/overview), [Rive: agents](https://rive.app/docs/cli/agents)). The verdict is where it goes, not whether it is good: a non-interactive dark hero wastes a state-machine runtime and pays at least 315 KB of WASM on the critical path, while a Jitter video does the same job at zero runtime; the in-app moments the UI plan names (set ticked, PR burst, rest timer, finish summary) are stateful and reused across sessions, so the download amortises there, but that is a separate decision requiring the app-wide `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` change, self-hosted WASM through `RuntimeLoader.setWasmUrl`, a Cadet plan for splash-free exports and a real-device measurement with `canvas-lite` (inference).

Spline is the one to refuse outright. Its runtime entry on bundlephobia is **270.3 KB gzipped** before chunks of 181.8, 147.5, 133.7 and 50.3 KB, a physics WASM of 586.7 KB and a `hana-ui` WASM of 1.10 MB; the drop-in `<spline-viewer>` script measured **987 KB gzipped** from unpkg, and an earlier 1.12.81 viewer build measured 638 KB over the wire plus 664 KB of physics ([bundlephobia @splinetool/runtime](https://bundlephobia.com/package/@splinetool/runtime), [unpkg spline-viewer.js](https://unpkg.com/@splinetool/viewer@latest/build/spline-viewer.js), [jsDelivr viewer 1.12.81](https://data.jsdelivr.com/v1/packages/npm/@splinetool/viewer@1.12.81)). Free "Web exports with watermark"; Hobby $15 a seat ($12 yearly) removes it; Pro $30 ($25) adds "Video export" and "Download Code Export"; "Self-Hosted Export" is listed under Enterprise while the runtime README says anyone can download and self-host the `.splinecode` file, a contradiction nobody can resolve without a paid account ([Spline pricing](https://spline.design/pricing), [@splinetool/runtime](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@splinetool/runtime)). Spline's own optimisation guide says to "only use one or two embeds per page" and to "avoid using multiple embeds on full-screen (the bigger the canvas, the more pixels are needed to render)" and publishes no mobile frame-rate or payload figures ([Spline: optimize your scene](https://docs.spline.design/f6351697797e4e41bbf57d62ab905a06)). None of the admired fitness sites references it, its own homepage loads 12 MP4 videos alongside the runtime, and the design direction already excludes 3D worlds ([spline.design](https://spline.design/)). The official MCP runs on Windows from the desktop app and does not change the runtime cost on the page ([Spline MCP](https://docs.spline.design/generate/spline-mcp-server)); if the owner wants a Spline-made glass render, Pro's video export turns it into media the page can carry (inference).

"LottieFiles" hides two products with opposite verdicts. The old `lottie-web` player (5.13.0, May 2025, MIT) renders JSON to SVG with no WASM: `lottie_light.min.js` is **46.4 KB gzipped**, the full build 76.0 KB, and the maintained `ngx-lottie` 22.0.0 (June 2026) wraps it for Angular 22 ([unpkg lottie_light](https://unpkg.com/lottie-web@5.13.0/build/player/lottie_light.min.js), [npm ngx-lottie](https://registry.npmjs.org/ngx-lottie)). The modern `.lottie` format and its dotLottie player are a Rust-and-WASM engine: 32.9 KB of JavaScript plus a **496.3 KB gzipped** `dotlottie-player.wasm` fetched from jsDelivr unless `setWasmUrl()` is called first, with a same-origin worker as the documented strict-CSP path, and no `@lottiefiles/dotlottie-angular` package exists ([bundlephobia dotlottie-web](https://bundlephobia.com/package/@lottiefiles/dotlottie-web), [jsDelivr dotlottie-web 0.81.0](https://data.jsdelivr.com/v1/packages/npm/@lottiefiles/dotlottie-web@0.81.0?structure=flat), [dotlottie-web README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LottieFiles/dotlottie-web/main/packages/web/README.md)). Only the first is admissible under the current policy, and even it must inject its SVG in `afterNextRender` into a pre-sized container and check `matchMedia` itself, since neither player honours reduced motion (inference from the Angular guide and the players' APIs). The pricing is Individual $19.99 a user a month billed yearly and Team $24.99, with a free workspace limited to "up to 5 private files" ([LottieFiles pricing](https://lottiefiles.com/pricing), [LottieFiles free limits](https://help.lottiefiles.com/hc/en-us/articles/16240626895385-file-upload-and-download-limits-on-free-workspace)). The Lottie Simple License grants the marketplace's public files "free of charge" "including for commercial purposes" with attribution "permitted" but not required ([Lottie Simple License](https://lottiefiles.com/page/license)); that a free, attribution-free library of hundreds of thousands of files reads as stock on a homepage is this report's inference, not a cited finding, and on a dark photographic hero any vector animation competes with the photograph. LottieFiles ships an official hosted MCP (`https://mcp.lottiefiles.com/mcp`, OAuth) whose four tools are a GraphQL workbench over the account's workspaces, and a local Creator MCP that needs a Creator browser tab open for the whole session; neither puts an animation on a page ([LottieFiles MCP](https://docs.lottiefiles.com/en/platform/mcp), [Lottie Creator MCP](https://docs.lottiefiles.com/en/creator/13_ai-tools/lottie-creator-mcp)).

## No connector makes the page prettier; two free ones make the agent faster

The mechanics bound what an unattended runner can use. Claude Code adds MCP servers with `claude mcp add --transport http <name> <url>` or `claude mcp add --transport stdio <name> -- npx -y <pkg>`, at local, project (`.mcp.json`) or user scope; OAuth is completed by `/mcp` in a session or `claude mcp login <name>` in a shell, and the docs describe no way to finish it inside `claude -p`; connectors added at claude.ai "appear automatically" for claude.ai-subscription sign-ins; project-scope servers "load without a prompt" in `claude -p`; and "servers that fetch external content can expose you to prompt injection risk" ([Claude Code MCP docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/mcp)). Plugins already installed load in `claude -p` even though `/plugin` does not run there, and `claude plugin install <name>@<marketplace>` works from a shell ([Claude Code plugins](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/discover-plugins)); Anthropic "does not control what MCP servers, files, or other software are included in plugins" from the `external_plugins` folder ([claude-plugins-official](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-plugins-official)). This session observed the consequence first-hand: the claude.ai Claude Docs and Google Drive connectors loaded headless, while the installed Figma plugin was flagged as needing an OAuth sign-in it cannot perform. The UI plan already sets the scope rule for this repo: tools go in "at user scope, not in the repo; a connector that needs a sign-in, such as Figma, cannot be used unattended" (`docs/UI-REDESIGN-PLAN.md` line 114), so the two installs below belong at user scope rather than in a committed `.mcp.json` (none exists today; the repo's `.claude` folder holds only `skills`).

What the repo already holds is the strongest free verification stack available. The Chrome DevTools MCP plugin (`chrome-devtools-mcp@chrome-devtools-plugins`, Apache-2.0, no account) exposes 59 tools, among them `emulate` with `Slow 4G`, `cpuThrottlingRate` and `prefersReducedMotion`, `take_snapshot` for the accessibility tree, and `lighthouse_audit`, which returns "accessibility, SEO, best practices, and agentic browsing. Performance is excluded, so run performance_start_trace for that instead" ([Chrome DevTools MCP tool reference](https://github.com/ChromeDevTools/chrome-devtools-mcp/blob/main/docs/tool-reference.md)); the phone performance number therefore keeps coming from the Lighthouse CLI, which "runs locally" and needs Node 22 ([GoogleChrome/lighthouse](https://github.com/GoogleChrome/lighthouse)). `@axe-core/playwright` 4.13.0 and `@playwright/test` 1.63.0 are in `src/web/package.json`, Playwright's `toHaveScreenshot()` gives pixel baselines without a service ([Playwright snapshots](https://playwright.dev/docs/test-snapshots)), and the repo skills `angular-developer` and `ui-review` (a pinned copy of Vercel's Web Interface Guidelines) carry the design voice. Nothing in the verification market adds to that for one page: Deque's Axe MCP Server publishes no install steps, auth or price ("Schedule a Demo") ([Deque axe MCP](https://www.deque.com/axe/mcp-server/)), BrowserStack's needs a purchased plan and OAuth ([browserstack/mcp-server](https://github.com/browserstack/mcp-server)), and Microsoft's Playwright MCP duplicates the DevTools server while shipping `browser_run_code_unsafe`, "the equivalent of remote code execution" ([microsoft/playwright-mcp](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright-mcp)).

Two additions are cheap, official and need no sign-in. The Angular CLI itself contains an MCP server, `npx -y @angular/cli mcp`, with `get_best_practices`, `search_documentation` over angular.dev, `devserver_start/stop/wait_for_build` and a `--read-only` flag that keeps to tools that leave the project unchanged ([angular.dev/ai/mcp](https://angular.dev/ai/mcp)); the repo's Angular skill already documents its JSON config. Context7 is in Anthropic's official marketplace, connects anonymously with shared rate limits or with a free key good for 1,000 calls a month (Pro is $10 a seat), and indexes GSAP as `/greensock/gsap` with 1,022 snippets ([external_plugins/context7](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-plugins-official/tree/main/external_plugins/context7), [Context7 plans](https://context7.com/plans), [Context7 GSAP](https://context7.com/greensock/gsap)); its risk is that indexed docs are third-party text, mitigated by read-only tools. GSAP's official skills are the third item, free and MIT. A fourth, optional and free, is LottieFiles' "philosophy-first, implementation-agnostic" motion-design skill, which works with CSS and GSAP but is four commits old and silent on reduced motion ([LottieFiles/motion-design-skill](https://github.com/LottieFiles/motion-design-skill)). The one connector that maps to "more premium and pretty" is Mobbin's official MCP, whose `search_sections` returns screenshots of shipped hero, pricing and footer sections, read-only, at 60 requests a minute; it needs a paid Mobbin plan and a one-time interactive OAuth, and its value is modest now that the 60-site research and the direction decision are done ([Mobbin MCP](https://mobbin.com/mcp)).

Everything else on the design side fails one of three tests. Output shape: 21st.dev's "10,000+ React and Tailwind components" ([21st-dev/magic-mcp](https://github.com/21st-dev/magic-mcp)), shadcn's registry organised around `@shadcn/react` ([shadcn MCP](https://ui.shadcn.com/docs/mcp)), v0's "Next.js, Tailwind, shadcn/ui" ([v0 docs](https://v0.app/docs)), Framer's unofficial relay with paid React export ([Framer plugin 446](https://www.framer.com/marketplace/plugins/446/)), Stitch's raw HTML screens, and Figma's `get_design_context`, whose default "is React + Tailwind" ([Figma tools](https://developers.figma.com/docs/figma-mcp-server/tools-and-prompts/)); transliterating any of these into Angular + SCSS costs more than writing the section and pulls the page toward the stock look the visual research rejected (inference). Auth and quota: Figma is OAuth-only, allows "20/month" tool calls on Starter and View/Collab seats against 200 a day on a $12-a-month Professional Dev seat, and in Claude Code its `get_screenshot` "can only describe screenshots rather than return them" ([Figma rate limits](https://developers.figma.com/docs/figma-mcp-server/rate-limits-access/), [Figma pricing](https://www.figma.com/pricing/), [Figma connector](https://claude.com/marketplace/connectors/figma)); the repo retired Figma as its source of truth on 2026-09-28 (project memory), so the installed plugin stays unauthorized unless the owner revives it. Stitch's API-key header path was reported broken in Claude Code (issue #41664, closed "not planned" into #3273) and its only wrapper is marked inactive ([claude-code#41664](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/41664), [Glama Stitch listing](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/huyy6ibnmw)). Wrong product: Webflow's MCP operates Webflow sites ([webflow/mcp-server](https://github.com/webflow/mcp-server)), Canva's connector produces raster designs behind a required sign-in ([Canva connector](https://claude.com/marketplace/connectors/canva)), and Adobe's connector "doesn't expose text-to-image or text-to-video generation directly" and has no documented Claude Code support ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/feature-requests-405/expose-firefly-generation-image-video-via-mcp-api-so-it-can-be-driven-from-claude-and-other-ai-agents-1637240)). The media and stock servers fail on the site's own rules: Cloudinary's five official servers, Mux's hosted server (whose `execute` tool has "no special protection for destructive operations") and ImageKit's would move images to hosts `img-src 'self' data:` does not allow ([cloudinary/mcp-servers](https://github.com/cloudinary/mcp-servers), [Mux MCP](https://mux.com/docs/integrations/mcp-server), [ImageKit MCP](https://imagekit.io/docs/mcp-server)); Unsplash and Pexels have no official server, their community wrappers need API keys in config, and neither licence says anything about model releases, which is the owner's real constraint for people photos ([Unsplash License](https://unsplash.com/license), [Pexels License](https://www.pexels.com/license/)); image-generation wrappers for Replicate and fal are community code with API keys and serve a use (AI-made people) the public page bans ([mcpservers.org listing](https://mcpservers.org/servers/GMKR/mcp-imagegen)). ## First-party form, proxied Plausible and self-hosted media; everything else links out

Every service in this section was judged with one table: third-party-web's "average impact", the mobile main-thread time an entity adds to pages that include it, computed from HTTP Archive's Lighthouse runs over about four million sites ([third-party-web](https://github.com/patrickhulce/third-party-web/blob/master/README.md)). The numbers make the premium and performance answers the same answer. Calendly adds **4,305 ms**, Intercom **1,433 ms**, HubSpot 1,226 ms, Osano's consent manager 1,706 ms, Cookiebot 398 ms, OneTrust 564 ms, Google reCAPTCHA 570 ms, Microsoft Clarity 405 ms, Google Tag Manager 1,066 ms, YouTube **6,297 ms**, Vimeo 4,099 ms and Wistia 5,003 ms, against Crisp at 33 ms, Fathom 59 ms and Google Analytics 108 ms ([third-party-web](https://github.com/patrickhulce/third-party-web/blob/master/README.md)). The 250 ms third-party audit threshold and the 30 % TBT weight mean the whole budget for vendor JavaScript on the homepage is smaller than any one of the booking, chat or consent widgets at its average, and under this CSP each of them is also a `script-src` plus `connect-src` and usually `frame-src` addition, often with `'unsafe-inline'` (inference from the directives). Intercom's own CSP article asks for `script-src 'nonce-{random}' 'unsafe-inline' 'unsafe-eval' 'strict-dynamic' https: http:` or an allowlist of roughly 70 hosts ([Intercom CSP](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/3894-using-intercom-with-content-security-policy)), and a hosting company's single-site test saw a marketing page drop from above 90 to 77 with the Messenger and recover when it loaded only after first interaction ([GreenGeeks](https://www.greengeeks.com/blog/ai-chatbot-website-loads-slow-fixes/)). A pre-rendered page shows whatever a vendor script draws only after hydration, so every embed is an empty box to crawlers and link previews unless a static fallback is in the HTML (inference from the Angular guides).

Contact for gym owners needs no vendor. A first-party "Talk to us" form (name, gym, phone or WhatsApp, message) posting to the existing API pre-renders fully, satisfies `form-action 'self'`, and is protected at launch volume by a honeypot field, a minimum-time check and nginx rate limiting (inference; standard practice). Beside it, a plain `<a href="https://wa.me/...">` link rather than a floating bubble: Lebanon had 5.38 million internet users at end-2025 (91.8 % penetration) ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-lebanon)) and Pew's 2019 survey found WhatsApp the most widely used platform there, a dated figure no newer source contradicts ([Pew Research](https://legacy.pewresearch.org/?p=323687)); the `wa.me` format itself could not be read from WhatsApp's page and is unverified here. If calendar self-booking is wanted, Cal.com is MIT-licensed, free for one user with Cal.com branding, and $12 a user a month on Teams to remove it; as a link from the form's thank-you step it costs nothing on the page, while an inline embed loads `app.cal.com/embed/embed.js` (24.1 KB measured) and would need `frame-src` and `script-src` changes ([Cal.com pricing](https://cal.com/pricing), [Cal.com LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/calcom/cal.com/main/LICENSE), [Cal.com embed](https://cal.com/docs/core-features/embed/adding-embed-to-your-webpage)). Calendly's Free plan cannot remove branding, Standard is $10 a seat, and its iframe is on `calendly.com` ([Calendly pricing](https://calendly.com/pricing), [Calendly embed](https://calendly.com/help/advanced-calendly-embed-for-developers)); a DebugBear-based audit once measured its widget CSS at 464 KB of Base64 fonts, whereas the research pass measured the current `widget.css` at 795 bytes compressed, so that figure describes an older build ([corewebvitals.io](https://corewebvitals.io/pagespeed/speed-up-calendly-integration)). If spam ever appears, Cloudflare Turnstile is the CAPTCHA to reach for: two directives, one host (`script-src` and `frame-src https://challenges.cloudflare.com`), nonce and `strict-dynamic` support, and pre-clearance that calls `/cdn-cgi/` on the site's own domain ([Turnstile CSP](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/reference/content-security-policy/)); hCaptcha wants four directives with wildcard hosts and a 104 KB script ([hCaptcha docs](https://docs.hcaptcha.com/)), and reCAPTCHA brings Google's EU consent policy with its 570 ms ([Google EU User Consent Policy](https://www.google.com/about/company/user-consent-policy/)). Hosted form builders are link-outs at most: Tally is free with no caps and EU-hosted, $24 a month to remove branding ([Tally pricing](https://tally.so/pricing)); Fillout's CAPTCHA is paid-only ([Fillout pricing](https://www.fillout.com/pricing)); Typeform starts at $39 a month for 100 responses ([Typeform pricing](https://www.typeform.com/pricing/)).

Interactive demos, chat and testimonial widgets are all "avoid" on this page for the same three reasons: iframe or vendor script, blank in the pre-render, watermarked or empty at launch. Arcade's free tier allows one published demo with a watermark that cannot be removed and Growth is $50 a seat ([Arcade pricing](https://arcade.software/pricing)); Storylane allows "1 interactive demo live at any given time" free and $50 a month on Starter ([Storylane pricing](https://www.storylane.io/pricing)); Navattic's free plan is one demo ([Navattic pricing](https://www.navattic.com/pricing)); Supademo's free plan allows five demos and exports to video or PDF, which is useful privately for the partner meeting without touching the page ([Supademo pricing](https://supademo.com/pricing)). For a page whose promise is the real app, a self-recorded MP4/WebM of the Atlas demo from the site's own origin or a "Take the 3-minute tour" link to a vendor-hosted page beats an embed (inference). Chat gets no bubble at launch because the admired pages have none and because Intercom costs $29 a seat plus the CSP above ([Intercom pricing](https://www.intercom.com/pricing)); if support chat is ever needed inside the signed-in app, Crisp is the lightest loader (2.9 KB measured, EU hosting, free for two seats) ([Crisp pricing](https://crisp.chat/en/pricing/)) and Chatwoot is MIT and self-hostable on an owned subdomain ([Chatwoot pricing](https://www.chatwoot.com/pricing), [Chatwoot LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chatwoot/chatwoot/develop/LICENSE)); Tawk's "Free Forever" chat charges $29 a month to remove its branding ([tawk.to pricing](https://www.tawk.to/pricing/)). Testimonials are a legal matter before they are a widget: the FTC's final rule bans fake reviews and testimonials "including AI-generated ones" and testimonials from people with no experience of the business, with civil penalties for knowing violators ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)); Senja (free for 15 testimonials) and Testimonial.to (free for 10 text and 2 video) can collect real quotes later, and the honest route is to paste consented text and photos into first-party HTML rather than load a widget ([Senja pricing](https://senja.io/pricing), [Testimonial.to pricing](https://testimonial.to/pricing)); Trustpilot's testimonial TrustBoxes are "Premium & Enterprise only" and its Starter plan is $99 a month paid yearly upfront ([Trustpilot pricing](https://business.trustpilot.com/pricing)).

Analytics is the one service to adopt now, and it can be adopted with no header change. Plausible "does not use cookies, browser cache or local storage", counts visitors by a daily-rotated salted hash, stores data in the EU, and states "you do not need cookie banners for analytics" ([Plausible data policy](https://plausible.io/data-policy)); its proxy guide routes the script and the event endpoint through the site's own domain, making them "indistinguishable from your own files", which keeps `script-src 'self'` and `connect-src 'self'` intact ([Plausible proxy](https://plausible.io/docs/proxy/introduction)); the script measured 1,361 bytes compressed against 155,532 for GA4's `gtag.js`, and Plausible's Starter is $9 a month ([Plausible](https://plausible.io/), [Plausible: lightweight analytics](https://plausible.io/lightweight-web-analytics)). Fathom ($15 a month, "No cookies, so no consent banner for analytics") and self-hosted Umami (MIT, free) are the equal alternatives ([Fathom pricing](https://usefathom.com/pricing), [Umami pricing](https://umami.is/pricing)). The rest set identifiers: GA4 sets `_ga` cookies with a two-year default ([Google Analytics cookies](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/11397207)); Clarity sets first- and third-party cookies including an advertising `MUID`, has enforced consent signals for EEA, UK and Swiss visits since 31 October 2025, and its CSP example puts `'unsafe-inline'` in `default-src` ([Clarity consent mode](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/clarity/setup-and-installation/cookie-consent), [Clarity cookies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/clarity/setup-and-installation/cookie-list), [Clarity CSP](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/clarity/setup-and-installation/clarity-csp)); PostHog Cloud is free for 1M events and 5k recordings a month but ships a 105 KB script and tells EU users to capture only with consent, which makes it an app-side tool with `persistence: 'memory'` until consent rather than a homepage one ([PostHog self-host](https://posthog.com/docs/self-host), [PostHog GDPR](https://posthog.com/docs/privacy/gdpr-compliance), [PostHog persistence](https://posthog.com/docs/libraries/js/persistence)). The consent consequence follows directly: with only cookieless first-party analytics the vendors say no banner is needed, the ICO's PECR guidance turns on storing or accessing information on the device ([ICO cookies](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/cookies-and-similar-technologies/)), CNIL exempts audience measurement that serves the publisher exclusively with anonymous statistics ([CNIL audience measurement](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-et-autres-traceurs/regles/cookies-solutions-pour-les-outils-de-mesure-daudience)), and Lebanon's Law 81/2018 has "no specific mention" of cookies and "no National Data Protection Authority" ([DLA Piper: Lebanon](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?t=law&c=LB)); the open legal point is the EDPB's 2/2023 guideline on what counts as "access", whose PDF could not be read ([EDPB guidelines 2/2023](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-22023-technical-scope-art-53-eprivacy-directive_en)). The moment a cookie-setting tool enters, a banner is mandatory for EEA, UK and Swiss visitors, and the lightest option measured is Cookiebot (free for one domain, EUR 7 a month on Lite, 37.6 KB, about 400 ms) ([Cookiebot pricing](https://www.cookiebot.com/en/pricing/)).

Media, fonts and the changelog stay first-party. The build already runs post-steps after `ng build`, so an encoding step (AVIF/WebP pictures, a 6-12 s muted WebM/MP4 with a poster) belongs there, served with long-lived immutable cache headers; `media-src` falls back to `default-src 'self'`, so no CSP change (inference from the build script and the directive). A CDN becomes a deliberate change only if traffic or geography demands it: Bunny's standard tier is $0.01 per GB in Europe and **$0.06 per GB in the Middle East and Africa** with a $1 monthly minimum ([Bunny pricing](https://bunny.net/pricing/)); Cloudflare Stream is $5 per 1,000 minutes stored and $1 per 1,000 minutes delivered with no free tier ([Cloudflare Stream pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/stream/pricing/)); Mux's player alone measured 310 KB compressed ([Mux pricing](https://www.mux.com/pricing)); Cloudinary's free tier excludes video bandwidth and its first paid plan is $99 a month ([Cloudinary pricing](https://cloudinary.com/pricing)). Fonts cost nothing: IBM Plex is SIL OFL 1.1 and already self-hosted as subsets ([IBM Plex LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IBM/plex/master/LICENSE.txt)); Lucide is ISC and Phosphor MIT when inlined as SVG ([Lucide license](https://lucide.dev/license), [Phosphor LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/phosphor-icons/core/main/LICENSE)); a premium grotesk later is a one-off self-hosted WOFF2 licence (Pangram Pangram's Neue Montreal, the face Eight Sleep uses, "starts at $40"; Klim's prices did not render) and never an Adobe Typekit or Google Fonts link, which Whoop and the coaching sites load from third-party origins ([Pangram Pangram](https://pangrampangram.com/products/neue-montreal), [Klim](https://klim.co.nz/buy/soehne/), [Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/)). A `/changelog` page generated at build time from a markdown file, with the newest date printed on the homepage from the same source, is the free, crawlable proof-of-life device; Headway's free widget (6.9 KB) and Beamer (free to 1,000 MAU with a watermark, inline config script) are in-app announcement tools for later, and Canny's Pro "starts at" $79 a month ([Headway](https://headwayapp.co/), [Beamer pricing](https://www.getbeamer.com/pricing), [Canny pricing](https://canny.io/pricing)). 3D flourishes end where Spline did: Vectary's unbranded viewer is a paid add-on ([Vectary pricing](https://www.vectary.com/pricing)), and the only WebGL option that fits the CSP is Unicorn Studio, whose roughly 50 KB gzipped SDK and scene JSON can be self-hosted with `data-us-lazyload` and `data-us-fps` knobs, but it needs the $168-a-year Legend plan for commercial use, cannot be pre-rendered, and `data-us-disablemobile` only disables interaction rather than rendering, so it is a wide-screen-only "later" with a static image fallback at best ([Unicorn Studio embed](https://unicorn.studio/docs/embed), [Unicorn Studio pricing](https://www.unicorn.studio/docs/pricing/)).

## Placeholders: Firefly for a month, marked files, a build that refuses them

The placeholders have one audience (the partner), one venue (a private build) and one rule (never public), so the tool choice is about friction and privacy, not quality. Adobe Firefly Standard is US$11.79 a month including VAT for 2,000 credits with unlimited basic image generation, outputs Adobe calls "safe for commercial use" because they are trained on licensed and public-domain material, and files that "automatically" carry Content Credentials ([Adobe Firefly plans](https://www.adobe.com/products/firefly/plans.html)); Adobe's guidelines forbid "Impersonating other people or entities" and removing or altering those credentials ([Adobe gen-AI guidelines](https://www.adobe.com/legal/licenses-terms/adobe-gen-ai-user-guidelines.html)). Google's Nano Banana Pro (Gemini 3 Pro Image) is $0.134 per 1K or 2K image on the API, Nano Banana 2 is $0.067, Google AI Studio use is free, image models are "Not available" on the free API tier, and SynthID watermarks are "added the moment content is created" ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing), [SynthID](https://deepmind.google/technologies/synthid/)); Google's policy bans impersonation "without explicit disclosure, in order to deceive" ([Google prohibited use policy](https://policies.google.com/terms/generative-ai/use-policy?hl=en-US)). Midjourney gives the most photographic results at $10 to $120 a month, but Stealth mode "is available only to Pro and Mega subscribers" ($60 and up) and covers midjourney.com only, which matters because every image on a cheaper plan is public, and its terms could not be read directly ([Midjourney plans](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/27870484040333-Comparing-Midjourney-Plans), [Midjourney Stealth](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/32019750070669-Stealth-Mode)). Open FLUX models are the cheapest (Replicate: flux-1.1-pro $0.04, flux-dev $0.025, flux-schnell $0.003 per image; fal FLUX.2 pro $0.03 per megapixel) but need prompt comfort and carry non-commercial licence terms on some weights ([Replicate pricing](https://replicate.com/pricing), [fal FLUX.2 pro](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/flux-2-pro/edit), [BFL pricing](https://bfl.ai/pricing)); OpenAI's API prices gpt-image-1.5 at $32 per million output tokens, roughly $0.009 to $0.133 an image by a third-party calculator ([OpenAI API pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)), and its usage policies ban impersonation and misrepresenting provenance ([OpenAI usage policies](https://openai.com/policies/usage-policies/)). Leonardo's and Ideogram's official pricing pages were unreachable, so their figures are third-party and conflicting. The recommendation is Firefly for one month, Nano Banana Pro in AI Studio for edits that need the same coach and client across several scenes, and Midjourney only if the first two look too clean and only on a Stealth-capable plan (inference). Never feed a real coach's or client's photo as a reference, never prompt for recognisable athletes, and keep every output at the scale it will be shown: consumer guides list the tells as hands, garbled lettering on apparel and signs, airbrushed skin, shadows that do not trace to one light, and backgrounds whose geometry fails, all more visible at full resolution ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/ai/ai-art/how-to-spot-ai-images)). The prompting practice the research pass used (name a real camera, lens and light source, ask for unposed mid-movement with sweat and chalk, ban "beautiful", "8k" and "cinematic", generate several and reject any with text or hands on bars) is practice guidance, not a cited finding.

The never-ship workflow uses hooks the repo already has. `angular.json` copies everything under `public` into the build with the glob `**/*` (`src/web/angular.json` lines 33-37), and `public/` today holds only `favicon.ico`, `fonts`, `hero-lab.css`, `icons`, `images`, `manifest.webmanifest` and `robots.txt`; stand-ins therefore live in a sibling folder such as `src/web/placeholders/` that the production asset glob never touches, added to the build only by a separate `placeholders` configuration (inference on the configuration name; the glob behaviour is verified). Each file carries a visible corner mark ("PLACEHOLDER - NOT FOR PUBLICATION") baked into the pixels and an XMP marker set with exiftool (for example `-XMP-dc:Rights="TB Gym placeholder - do not publish" -XMP-dc:Source="AI placeholder"`; the exact exiftool syntax was not verified in this pass), and Firefly files already carry a C2PA manifest that over 500 companies' tooling can read ([contentcredentials.org](https://contentcredentials.org/)). The failing check extends `scripts/check-prerender.mjs`, which `npm run build:check` runs on every `npm run build` and which already refuses an inline script or a linked stylesheet in `index.html`: it should also fail when any file under `dist/` has `placeholders/` in its path or contents, when any image in `dist/` carries the XMP marker or a C2PA manifest, or when any `<img>`, `<source>` or `<video>` in the pre-rendered HTML points outside the hashed asset set (the C2PA detection method is unverified). Because `scripts/check.ps1` runs `npm run format:check`, `npm run lint` and `npm run build` for the web side (`scripts/check.ps1` lines 121-128), a commit cannot pass with a placeholder referenced. The private version is served from a separate hostname behind nginx basic auth that sends `X-Robots-Tag: noindex`, so it cannot be indexed even if a link leaks, and the meeting deck says plainly that the people are stand-ins, which also keeps the page clear of the FTC's ban on AI-generated testimonials: no quote, rating or outcome is ever attached to a placeholder person ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). The alternatives for the same meeting without any AI image are people-free environment shots from the free libraries (no release needed) or released stock from Stocksy at $35-135 an image, which the earlier visual research already cleared ([Photutorial on Stocksy](https://photutorial.com/stocksy-review), [Unsplash releases](https://help.unsplash.com/en/articles/2612329-releases-and-trademark)).

## Conclusion

The question "which connector makes the homepage more premium" has a structural answer: the connector market sells generation (React components, Figma context, Stitch screens), editing (Rive, Lottie Creator, Spline, Canva) and verification (DevTools, Playwright, axe), and none of those three categories is where the admired pages' quality lives. Their quality is in media they commissioned and in a disciplined first-party build, which is why Apple Fitness+ reaches the bar with zero third parties and Oura with no video at all. The strict CSP TB Gym chose turns out to be a design advantage rather than a handicap: it makes every "add a snippet" shortcut a visible decision, and the shortcuts it blocks (chat bubbles, booking grids, demo iframes, consent banners, WASM runtimes) are precisely the coaching competitors' tells.

Two things change for the roadmap. First, GSAP's 2025 licence change means the whole professional motion toolset (SplitText, Flip, MorphSVG) is now free and already installed, so the "three or four short scroll stages" need no new dependency and no CSP change; the budget that might have gone to a motion subscription goes to the shoot and one month of Jitter. Second, the Rive decision should be re-framed from "homepage" to "in-app success states", where its state machines and one-time WASM download make sense, and taken together with the app-wide `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` question and a real-device measurement rather than inherited from a homepage tool list. Everything else in the five-tool answer and the connector directory can be declined on evidence, which is a better place to be than declining it on taste.
