# TB Gym homepage shoot: brief for the photographer and videographer

Drafted 2026-10-12 for the homepage rebuild (UI plan R6.2d-g). The shoot is not booked yet; this
brief is what a Beirut photographer or small production company quotes against. Decisions that are
the owner's are marked **owner**.

## What TB Gym is, in one paragraph

TB Gym is a web app for personal trainers, coaches and gyms. A coach builds a client's training
program and meal plan, the client logs every set on their phone, answers a weekly check-in and
messages the coach inside the app, and both of them see the same week. The homepage will show that
week from both sides with real screens of the app. The photographs and the short film supply the
people and the place; the app's screens are supplied by TB Gym and composited later.

## The look we want

Premium, serious, quiet. Think of Future's homepage (a sweating athlete on a grey-lilac backdrop,
one light headline), Peloton's 2023 member films (real people in their real places, documentary,
before-during-after a workout) and Eight Sleep's low-key, warm, single-source light. Not the
bright, smiling, over-lit "fitness stock" look. Nobody looks at the camera. Nobody poses.

- Light: low-key, one dominant source, warm skin, deep shadows, no coloured gels, no neon.
- Grade: natural, slightly warm, with true blacks. Deliver flat/log for video so the grade is ours.
- Lens: 35-50 mm equivalents for people and place; a short telephoto for hands and screens.
- Environment: a real gym with a rack, a platform and a desk or bench where a coach works on a
  laptop or phone. Dawn or evening light through real windows if the gym has them.
- Wardrobe: solid, muted colours. No logos, no slogans, no lime green, no all-black outfits that
  disappear into the shadows.
- Devices: current-generation iPhone and a laptop. TB Gym's screens will be placed in post (see
  "Screens" below), so the device on set shows a dark or grey screen with markers.

## Who is in the pictures

Two real coaches and one client, cast for believability rather than physique: people who train
and coach, 25-45, with a mix of builds and backgrounds. **Owner:** whether they are TB Gym's own
early coaches or hired talent. Everyone signs a release before the shoot (see "Releases").

## The shot list

The first two are the hero candidates and get the controlled light. Everything else is documentary:
follow the coach and the client through a real session and shoot what happens.

1. **The coach reads a check-in at the rack.** Coach standing beside a loaded barbell, phone in
   hand, reading. Low-key, one source from the side. Horizontal and vertical. This is the hero.
2. **The coach builds the week.** At a desk or bench, laptop open, the gym behind them out of
   focus. Over-the-shoulder and three-quarter.
3. **The client logs a set.** Between sets, breathing, phone in hand, the bar behind. Close on the
   hands and phone, and wider with the face.
4. **The lift.** The client under the bar, the coach watching from the side. Mid-rep and the
   moment after.
5. **Coach and client together.** Talking at the bar, looking at the phone together, no camera
   awareness.
6. **The team.** Two coaches on the floor at once, each with their own client, for the "gyms and
   coaching teams" section.
7. **The place.** The empty rack at dawn, chalk on the bar, plates, the desk with the laptop. No
   people. These also work as backgrounds for text.
8. **Hands and screens.** Thumb on the phone, the laptop keyboard, a hand on the bar. Tight.

## Film

- 10-15 second loop-safe takes of shots 1, 3, 4 and 5, each in 16:9 and 9:16. Slow, steady camera
  (tripod or gimbal), no whip pans, no speed ramps. Start and end on a hold so the loop cuts clean.
- No audio is needed; we ship the loop muted.
- 4K, 24 or 25 fps, log or flat profile. We deliver the final loop at 1280x720 and 1080x1920 under
  2 MB each, so the picture must read at that size: one subject, one movement.

## Screens

The app's screens must be exact, so they are placed in post. Two ways, the first preferred:

- **Screen replacement.** Shoot the phone and laptop with the screen dark or at about 20 % grey,
  with five small tracking markers on the glass. Brace the device or keep the hand still. Keep
  the device in focus (no shallow focus on the phone). TB Gym supplies the screens at 3x.
- **Live screen.** Only for static, clamped shots: balance flash and screen exposure so both read.

## Deliverables

- 30 retouched selects as full-resolution TIFF or 16-bit PSD plus web JPEG, with the RAW files.
- The film takes as ProRes or high-bitrate H.265, with a first-frame still for each.
- Naming: `tbgym-<shot>-<take>-<orientation>`.
- Delivery within 10 working days of the shoot. **Owner:** the date.

## Releases and rights

- Every person signs a model release: worldwide, web and social, perpetual, including composites
  and screen replacement, compensation stated. The gym signs a property release.
- TB Gym owns the pictures for its own marketing. The photographer may use them in a portfolio.
- Nobody photographed is described on the site as a customer unless they are one and say so in
  writing. The pictures are photography, not testimony.
- No AI-generated or AI-altered people, ever. Retouching is limited to colour, dust and small
  distractions.

## Budget and quotes

No Lebanese rate card was found during the research. Benchmarks to quote against: an established
commercial photographer at £500-1,000 or $1,000-3,000 per day plus £10-25 per retouched image; a
60-second brand film averaging about $3,200; talent at roughly $300-1,400 per person per day; Beirut
is described by a fixer as among the most competitive in the region. Ask three Beirut suppliers for
a one-day combined stills-and-video quote against this brief, with and without talent and the
location fee. **Owner:** the budget ceiling.

## Timeline

The homepage ships first with the product as the picture; the shoot's stills replace the hero and
the week's pictures when they arrive, and the loop is measured on a phone before it stays. So the
shoot can be booked when convenient; nothing on the site waits for it.
