# Real people and real screens make coaching pages premium

The pages the owner admires look premium because of what fills their first screen, not because of a layout trick: a real person or a real device at 50-64 % of the viewport width, one headline at 64-120 px in a light-to-medium weight, one sentence, one pill, and product UI treated as a photographed subject (a current-generation iPhone frame, a bare window with a hairline border, or a glass card lifted out of a photograph) with realistic data inside ([Trainerize](https://www.trainerize.com/), [Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/), [Future](https://www.future.co/), [Linear](https://linear.app/)). TB Gym's current page has the right story (one week of coaching seen from both sides) and passes the hard bar (**Lighthouse phone performance 90-91, accessibility 100** per [homepage-research.md](C:\Users\Admin\Desktop\TB gym\docs\design\homepage-research.md)), but it fails on exactly those surfaces: a CSS rectangle standing in for a phone at roughly 200 CSS px, below the ~320 px screen width at which app text stays legible; no photography at all on a page in a category where every leader but one shows real people; a cream page with a contained dark card instead of the dark hero and dark finale the fitness brands use; a 600-weight headline at -5.5 % tracking, heavier and tighter than Apple, Whoop or Oura; one pinned stage crossfading seven times, a structure none of the measured premium stories use; six numbered text cards, the pattern of the worst-read competitor pages; and a "who it's for" card pair that names audiences without showing either of them anything. The evidence supports one playbook that a software product without hardware can run in full, because Future already does at 7.6 MB with no animation library: a still or sub-2 MB loop of a person mid-effort, dark bookends around a cream body, Apple's own bezel over 3x captures or bezel-less crops, features as real UI crops under 3-7-word outcome headlines, audiences routed in the nav and a late story-led section rather than a hero toggle, and pre-customer proof made of a founder's note, product facts, a demo marked as sample and real counts, never invented quotes (fake testimonials have carried US civil penalties since October 2024). Of the three directions at the end, the recommendation is the photographic one, staged so the page ships on device pictures first and takes the commissioned shoot's stills and loop when they arrive.

## Premium is one headline, one sentence, one pill, on a person or a product

The seven premium fitness homepages captured on 2026-10-12 agree on the first screen to a degree the coaching platforms do not. Heroes fill **89-100 % of a 1440x900 viewport** (Whoop exactly 900 px, Oura 900 px, Peloton 852 px, Future 864 px, Apple Fitness+ 804 px), the only exceptions being Tonal's 600 px split and Eight Sleep's 1,421 px hero that runs past the fold ([Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/), [Oura](https://ouraring.com/), [Eight Sleep](https://www.eightsleep.com/), [Tonal](https://www.tonal.com/), [Peloton](https://www.onepeloton.com/), [Future](https://www.future.co/), [Apple Fitness+](https://www.apple.com/apple-fitness-plus/)). Five of the seven run a muted loop; Oura and Future use stills of **49 KB and 64 KB** and look no less premium, which is the single most useful fact for a page that must hold a 90 phone score ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/), [Future](https://www.future.co/)). Copy is one headline plus one sentence, and the first-screen CTA count is one pill on Whoop, Oura, Future and Apple, two on Tonal and Peloton, none on Eight Sleep until y=1,300 (captures of the sites above). The hardware brands lead with the object (Whoop's gold mesh macro, Tonal's arm, Eight Sleep's Hub rising into frame); the service brands lead with a person mid-effort or mid-life (Apple Fitness+'s two trainers under tungsten light, Peloton's close-up, Future's sweating athlete on a grey-lilac backdrop), and a software product belongs with the second group (inference from the same captures).

The coaching platforms the owner admires are, structurally, ordinary: every one splits the first screen into a text column and a media block, with a 6-13-word headline at 42-68 px, an email field joined to a button and a "no credit card" line ([Trainerize](https://www.trainerize.com/), [TrueCoach](https://truecoach.co/), [Everfit](https://everfit.io/)). What makes them read as "professional" is the media: Trainerize fills **64 % of the viewport** with a filmed iPhone on bedding beside a woman, TrueCoach photographs a coach holding a phone in a bright gym with UI cards composited around him, Everfit runs a strip of athlete and meal-prep photographs that changes with a rotating audience word ([Trainerize](https://www.trainerize.com/), [TrueCoach](https://truecoach.co/), [Everfit](https://everfit.io/)). Hevy Coach, which shows a flat dashboard screenshot and no people, reads as clean rather than premium ([Hevy Coach](https://hevycoach.com/)). The sizes of those media blocks also explain the load times: three of the five coaching sites never fired `load` within 45 s in any capture mode, which says their polish is bought with media weight, not with lighter pages (capture log for the sites above; inference). In a February 2026 sample of 28 SaaS homepages only **9.5 % of heroes used real people** and 57.1 % showed product UI in action, so a coaching page that does both is rare rather than conventional ([Tenet](https://www.wearetenet.com/blog/saas-hero-section-best-practices)); the same sample found only **60.7 % of hero titles understandable within five seconds**, the owner's own time limit ([Tenet](https://www.wearetenet.com/blog/saas-hero-section-best-practices)).

The typographic recipe is measurable. Whoop sets its hero at **120 px / weight 400 / -3 % tracking / line-height 0.8**, Oura at 80 px / 300 in the serif Editorial New with -5 % tracking, Apple at 80 px / 600 / -1.5 %, Future at 84-100 px / 400, Linear at 64-72 px / 510, Vercel at 64 px / 400 / -6 %; bodies sit at 15-18 px, and at 390 px the headlines drop to 38-56 px ([Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/), [Oura](https://ouraring.com/), [Apple MacBook Pro](https://www.apple.com/macbook-pro/), [Future](https://www.future.co/), [Linear](https://linear.app/), [Vercel](https://vercel.com/)). The "startup" look is equally concrete: Poppins or Inter Bold at 42-48 px with a 1.3 line-height (Trainerize 42 px / 700 / 54.6 px, TrueCoach 47.5 px / 700) or heavy condensed uppercase (Everfit 68 px, Tonal's GT Expanded at 900) ([Trainerize](https://www.trainerize.com/), [TrueCoach](https://truecoach.co/), [Everfit](https://everfit.io/), [Tonal](https://www.tonal.com/)). TB Gym's display today is `clamp(2.6rem, 4.7vw, 5rem)`, 41.6-80 px at weight 600 with -0.055em tracking and 0.98 line-height ([_brand.scss](C:\Users\Admin\Desktop\TB gym\src\web\src\styles\_brand.scss)); the size is right and the weight and tracking are not. Moving to IBM Plex Sans Medium (500) at -2.5 % to -3 % with line-height 1.0-1.04 lands inside the hardware-brand band without changing family (inference from the measurements). The serif needs a different job: Instrument Serif's ubiquity is now the subject of design commentary (a January 2026 piece documents its use by the White House, Hook and a run of AI brands and quotes its designer acknowledging it as the trend's most visible face), and the catalogue of AI-design tells lists "an oversized italic serif headline" and "cream and beige as a default substitute for a considered palette" ([atypeofamigo](https://atypeofamigo.com/outstanding-fonts-why-is-instrument-serif-conquering-the-world/), [Impeccable](https://www.impeccable.style/slop)). The pairing itself is still current (Oura's Editorial New plus Akkurat, Robinhood's Martina Plantijn), but the fashionable form has shifted to the serif as a whole display voice or as numerals and quotes; Eight Sleep gives numbers their own face (Oswald at 54-72 px) and Whoop's guidelines prescribe DIN for numerals ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/), [Robinhood](https://robinhood.com/us/en/), [Eight Sleep](https://www.eightsleep.com/), [Whoop brand guidelines PDF](https://developer.whoop.com/assets/files/WHOOP%20-%20Brand%20%26%20Design%20Guidelines-bdea3554e94b4ea09e68695b1e8dc8e7.pdf)). For TB Gym that means upright Instrument Serif for large numerals and pull-quotes, at most one italic word per headline and none in the hero, and IBM Plex Mono (same OFL family, zero licence cost) for the 12-13 px uppercase eyebrow voice that Linear (Berkeley Mono), Eight Sleep (IBM Plex Mono) and Tonal (GT Mono) use for labels ([IBM Plex](https://github.com/IBM/plex), [Instrument Serif](https://github.com/Instrument/instrument-serif)).

"Dark and premium" is also narrower than it looks. The near-blacks are achromatic or barely cast (Linear #08090a, Vercel #000, Apple #000 and #1d1d1f, Mercury #171721, Oura #1c1b1a, Tonal #1c1c1a); elevation comes from **1 px white hairlines at 8-14 % alpha and white-alpha surface overlays of 2-5 %**, never from coloured glows; glass (backdrop blur 8-42 px) is reserved for nav bars, hero inputs and one or two cards; nobody in the set uses film grain, noise, mesh gradients or neon ([Linear](https://linear.app/), [Vercel](https://vercel.com/), [Apple MacBook Pro](https://www.apple.com/macbook-pro/), [Mercury](https://mercury.com/), [Eight Sleep](https://www.eightsleep.com/)). Two rhythms exist, measured as the share of page height that is dark: developer tools and Apple product pages are **95-100 % dark**, while the fitness and wellness brands bookend a light or cream body with a dark hero and a dark finale, **Whoop 35 %, Future 35 %, Eight Sleep 26 %, Oura 21 %, Tonal 21 %**, against Trainerize 17 %, TrueCoach 9 % and Everfit 4 % (luminance bands of the full-page captures of the sites above). Whoop's hero is 900 px of black and its finale 1,700 px; Eight Sleep's hero runs 2,000 px; the dark middle sections are inset black panels with 24-32 px corners against white ([Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/), [Eight Sleep](https://www.eightsleep.com/)). A cream-bodied TB Gym with long dark bookends is therefore squarely inside the Whoop/Oura/Eight Sleep pattern; the current page, which puts its only dark surface in a contained card beside the headline, is not.

Green has its own evidence. Wise's Forest Green #163300 and Bright Green #9FE870 contrast at **9.45:1**, and TB Gym's forest #153d33 and lime #d9ed94 land at **9.44:1**, the same engineering ([Wise](https://wise.com/gb/blog/a-brand-for-everywhere-wise-unveils-bold-new-look), contrast computed with the WCAG 2.x formula, [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum)). The difference is use: Wise spends the pair on seventeen flat lime blocks, which reads friendly; Robinhood (#ccff00), Ladder (#e6ff00) and Everfit (#d5f840) use neon lime on black as large fills and glows, which now reads "sporty fintech/fitness" ([Wise](https://wise.com/), [Robinhood](https://robinhood.com/us/en/), [Ladder](https://www.joinladder.com/), [Everfit](https://everfit.io/)). The expensive greens use green as the dark neutral itself (Aston Martin's "black" token is the green-cast #161a11; British racing green is #004225) with off-white text and a desaturated accent ([Aston Martin](https://www.astonmartin.com/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_racing_green)). One number in particular should settle how lime is spent: Everfit's footer is #d4ed99, **four units from TB Gym's #d9ed94**, so any full-width lime band on TB Gym's page reads as Everfit ([Everfit](https://everfit.io/)). Used as text, numerals, eyebrow marks and a single pill on a green-cast night surface (#0a0f0d-#0f1513), the same lime stays a signal; the computed pairs clear AAA (lime on #0a0f0d 15.17:1, cream #fbfaf5 on night #0c1e19 16.53:1, secondary #a5b2ab on #0f1513 8.40:1) and the green-cast black escapes the "Linear look" that design commentary now names as the default output of AI tools (purple or cyan accents, blurred gradient blobs, glow borders, three identical cards with icon tiles) ([brainy.ink](https://brainy.ink/paper/why-every-saas-looks-the-same-2026), [925studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells), [Impeccable](https://www.impeccable.style/slop)).

## Phones read as real at 350 px in a current bezel, or with no bezel at all

The owner's verdict on the drawn phone ("just a rectangle, feels AI-made") matches what the captures show about every premium site: **none of the seven coaching sites and none of the seven premium brands draws a device with CSS** ([Trainerize](https://www.trainerize.com/), [Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/)). Three treatments carry the premium pages. Family composites one 762x1502 bezel PNG of 42,998 bytes (an iPhone 15 Pro-style frame with a transparent screen, Dynamic Island and side buttons) over 1170x2532 MP4 screen recordings, always front-on, at **392x773 CSS px** in feature rows, cropped by the card edge so the top 60 % shows ([Family](https://family.co/)). Future centres one silver-titanium iPhone frame at 360x727 CSS px playing a 634x1280 WebM of a real coach chat, and in the same section shows a coach's selfie check-in inside a second frame ([Future](https://www.future.co/)). Oura drops the device altogether: five bezel-less screens fanned in one 1440x763 PNG, the centre one largest, with big numerals ("Readiness 88", "STEPS 5,203") ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/)). Eight Sleep photographs a person with a phone and lifts one UI card out beside them ("96 SLEEP FITNESS SCORE", "SLEEP TEMPERATURE 63° / 77°") as a glass card over the photograph ([Eight Sleep](https://www.eightsleep.com/)). Linear and Mercury show bare dark windows with 1 px borders, cropped at the section edge, no browser chrome and no laptop ([Linear](https://linear.app/), [Mercury](https://mercury.com/)). The cheap-looking set shares the opposite traits: TrueCoach's hero is a collage of a tilted notch-era phone, a tablet, a bare desktop screenshot and store badges at mismatched scales, and its mid-page laptop mockup renders the dashboard at 792x380 CSS px where the text is not legible; Hevy's hero is a 1x composite that goes soft when enlarged; Strava's phone is about 210 px wide ([TrueCoach](https://truecoach.co/), [Hevy](https://www.hevy.com/), [Strava](https://www.strava.com/)). Even Whoop shows the failure mode: its own image alt texts read "Exported from Figma... Comp-resolution export - replace with production master when available" ([Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/)). What separates the two groups is not resolution but current bezel geometry or none, one phone shown large or deliberately cropped, everything front-on, the UI lit and backed like a subject, and assets served at 2x or more (inference from the captures above).

Legibility sets the size. On iPhone 17 and 17 Pro the logical screen is **402x874 points at 3x**, iOS body text defaults to 17 pt with an 11 pt floor, and Apple's marketing rules demand devices "no smaller than 200 px onscreen" ([iOS Ref](https://iosref.com/res), [Apple HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography), [Apple Marketing Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines/)). Shown-text size equals UI text size times screen CSS width divided by logical width, so 14 px UI text becomes 11.1 px at a 320 px screen (0.8x), 9.1 px at 260 px and 7.3 px at Strava's 210 px; the observed premium phones sit at 350-392 px and either crop the bottom or pick screens dominated by large numerals (inference; observed widths from [Family](https://family.co/) and [Future](https://www.future.co/)). TB Gym's hero phone measures roughly 200 CSS px wide in the 1440 px screenshot ([r62b-home-1440.png](C:\Users\Admin\Desktop\TB gym\docs\design\r62b-home-1440.png), approximate measurement), which puts its 14 px UI text near 7 px; no amount of bezel would fix that at that size.

Apple's rules decide which frame. The Marketing Guidelines require Apple-provided bezels "in all your marketing materials", the latest-generation device, images used "as is" with no added reflections, shadows, cropping, tilting or animation, the real running app on screen, and they list "Rendering in 3D or creating any simulation of an Apple product" and illustrations of Apple products under unauthorized uses ([Apple Marketing Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines/)). Apple's Design Resources publish free PSD/PNG bezels for iPhone 18, 17, 16 and iPhone Duo, the current MacBooks and Apple Watch ([Apple Design Resources](https://developer.apple.com/design/resources/#product-bezels)). Read literally, that rules out the two most common "premium" tricks, angled 3D renders (Rotato, Whoop's membership cards) and cropping at a card edge (Family, Future), and it means the strictly compliant look is Apple's iPhone 17 or 18 bezel, whole, front-on, no shadow on the device, with depth coming from the backdrop instead (inference). Two routes avoid the rules entirely because no Apple product is depicted: bezel-less screenshots (Oura's fan) and single UI cards over photography (Whoop, Eight Sleep, Everfit); both also make text larger for the same pixels. TB Gym has no store app, so the screen inside any bezel is the installed web app in standalone mode; whether a PWA satisfies "your app on the Apple devices it supports" is a grey area no source settles, and no source states whether Apple enforces the guidelines against ordinary SaaS pages, so the bezel-less routes are the safer default and the bezel route is a counsel question (inference from the guideline text).

The pipeline is cheap and repeatable on Windows, which matters because every UI change otherwise means new mockups. Capture with Playwright at `viewport 402x874, deviceScaleFactor 3`, light or dark scheme, timezone Asia/Beirut, logged in as the demo client ([Playwright emulation](https://playwright.dev/docs/emulation)); draw the 9:41 full-signal status bar and home indicator yourself (a web app has none; the convention is time 9:41, full bars, full battery) ([App Screenshot Studio](https://appscreenshotstudio.com/blog/iphone-app-store-screenshot-device-frame-status-bar-2026)); layer Apple's bezel PNG; export at 2x of the CSS slot as AVIF (96.35 % support) with WebP and PNG fallbacks in a `<picture>` element with `width`/`height`, `loading="lazy"` below the fold and `fetchpriority="high"` on the hero ([Can I use AVIF](https://caniuse.com/avif), [MDN picture](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/picture)). The admired sites ship such pictures at 30-160 KB (Whoop's phone render 41,694 bytes, Everfit's dashboard card 122,344 bytes, Mercury's feature image 142,737 bytes), so ten phone pictures cost roughly 0.5-1 MB, most of it lazy ([Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/), [Everfit](https://www.everfit.io/), [Mercury](https://mercury.com/); per-picture estimate is an inference from those sizes). The alternatives cost more and buy less: Rotato ($79-199 one-time) and Screen Studio ($9-29/month) are Mac-only and produce the 3D renders Apple's text rejects; Mockuuups is $3.90 per export or about $11-20 a month with a commercial licence and model-released people; Previewed is $9.99 for ten 1080p exports; LS Graphics sells an iPhone 17 pack for $39 ([Rotato](https://rotato.app/pricing), [Screen Studio](https://screen.studio/), [Mockuuups](https://mockuuups.studio/pricing/), [Previewed](https://previewed.app/pricing), [LS Graphics](https://www.ls.graphics/)). Do-it-yourself with Apple's free bezels is **$0-20 and about 12-16 hours**; a freelance designer producing 18 pictures from supplied 3x screenshots is roughly **$270-1,800** at $30-100 per hour ([Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/hire/ui-designers/cost/); the hour and total figures are the device note's inferences). For the coach's screen the readable choices are a bare window with a 1 px border and soft shadow at 1,100-1,300 CSS px, or a 1:1 crop of one meaningful region (a client's week, a program table) with one or two floated detail cards as Everfit and Mercury do; a MacBook render only makes sense when the laptop is itself the subject at 900+ px and the UI is simplified for the picture ([Everfit](https://www.everfit.io/), [Mercury](https://mercury.com/), [TrueCoach](https://truecoach.co/)). TB Gym's current flat crop is the right family; it lacks the window edge, the backdrop and a deliberate 1:1 crop on one region (inference from [r62b-home-1440.png](C:\Users\Admin\Desktop\TB gym\docs\design\r62b-home-1440.png)).

## Features get read as pictures with numbers; stories get read in short stages

Eyetracking explains why six numbered text cards go unread. Users spend about **57 % of page-viewing time above the fold, 74 % in the first two screenfuls and 81 % in the first three**, and more than 42 % within the top 20 % of the page ([NN/g scrolling](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)); they scan rather than read and need front-loaded, information-carrying words at the left edge ([NN/g F-pattern](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content-discovered/), [NN/g how people read](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-people-read-online/)); purely decorative images are "completely ignored" while information-carrying product pictures are "scrutinized" ([NN/g photos](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/)); digits stand out to peripheral vision before fixation ([NN/g numerals](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/web-writing-show-numbers-as-numerals/)); content behind tabs is overlooked and the default tab gets the attention ([NN/g tabs](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tabs-used-right/), [Baymard tabs](https://baymard.com/research-articles/avoid-horizontal-tabs)); auto-forwarding carousels and horizontal swipes are missed ([NN/g auto-forwarding](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/auto-forwarding/), [NN/g horizontal scrolling](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/horizontal-scrolling/)). The one controlled result in the set is blunt: replacing a hand-drawn illustration with screenshots of the UI on devices produced a **96 % increase in trial sign-ups**, and participants said the illustrations looked "ill-suited to an enterprise application" ([Conversion Rate Experts](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/statuscake-landing-page-win-report/)). Copy matters as much: in Unbounce's benchmark of 41,000 pages, SaaS copy at a 5th-7th grade level converted at **12.9 % against 2.1 % for professional-level copy**, simple copy converted 514 % better, the best SaaS pages ran 250-725 words, and 79 % of visits were on phones ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/saas-conversion-rate/)).

Nine feature patterns are in live use, and the premium brands converge on one grammar whatever the pattern: an optional eyebrow or tag, a **3-7-word outcome headline, at most one 12-25-word sentence, a picture that carries real numbers, and one quiet link** (inference from the captures below). Apple's "Get to know iPhone" tiles pair a category eyebrow with a 3-6-word headline and no body ("Chip and Battery Life — Fast that lasts.") ([Apple iPhone](https://www.apple.com/iphone/)); Tonal's rows are a 4:3 picture, a 4-6-word heading and a 14-29-word sentence with a concrete mechanism ("increasing in as little as one-pound increments") ([Tonal](https://www.tonal.com/)); Eight Sleep asks questions ("Intense workout? The Pod boosts your recovery.") over a photo with the app card overlaid ([Eight Sleep](https://www.eightsleep.com/)); Hevy Coach, the best-read coaching page, runs three rows of frameless real-UI crops on grey cards with 5-9-word headlines and 16-23-word sentences, showing "He lifted 8,762 kg over 24 sets" rather than claiming it ([Hevy Coach](https://hevycoach.com/)); PT Distinction's sixteen tiles are cropped real screens with real data ("Tyson Becks 100 %") ([PT Distinction](https://www.ptdistinction.com/)). The pages that read worst are the icon-and-text grids: TrueCoach's nine cards with a purple line icon and 27-47-word paragraphs, Kahunas' pastel icon tiles and 95-145-word bodies, TrainHeroic's three uppercase mottos with no body ([TrueCoach](https://truecoach.co/), [Kahunas](https://kahunas.io/), [TrainHeroic](https://www.trainheroic.com/)). The premium sites never prefix a feature with "01, 02"; numbering appears only in genuine step sequences (Peloton's three steps, Arcade's "how it works"), so TB Gym's numbered cards signal "list" where the page needs "story" ([Peloton](https://www.onepeloton.com/), [Arcade](https://www.arcade.software/)). Lists of capability names belong after the blocks, as Hevy Coach's twelve-name list and Vercel's four-name "Features" list do, not inside them ([Hevy Coach](https://hevycoach.com/), [Vercel](https://vercel.com/home)). The effort is small: **1-2 developer days for six alternating rows, 2-3 for a two-size tile grid** including the phone layout, against 3-4 days for tabs or accordions whose state and accessibility work buys hidden content (feature note estimates, inference). Interactive demo embeds are cheap at entry ($0-50 a month) but 88 % of their sessions happen on desktop and the embed renders as a blank frame to crawlers, previews and headless browsers, so they suit a "take a tour" link rather than the feature section ([Navattic report](https://navattic.com/report/state-of-the-interactive-product-demo-2025), [Arcade pricing](https://www.arcade.software/pricing)). One structural opportunity is specific to TB Gym: none of the coaching platforms shows the coach's side and the client's side of the same feature in one block, so a feature row that pairs the coach's screen with the client's phone for the same moment would be distinctive in this market (inference from the captures of the platforms above).

The scroll story needs a different structure, not more frames. Of 26 product sites captured, only Fitbod and Eight Sleep's "New to Pod 6" list pin a picture while copy changes; Linear's and Stripe's current homepages use static sections and tile grids ([Fitbod](https://fitbod.me/), [Eight Sleep](https://www.eightsleep.com/), [Linear](https://linear.app/), [Stripe](https://stripe.com/)). Apple's 2026 product pages, the owner's reference for "cinematic", contain **no canvas and no GSAP**: AirPods Pro 3 has 11 `<video>` elements with `preload="none"`, `muted`, `playsinline` and no autoplay attribute, each with start and end poster JPGs of 47-131 KB, played by Apple's own script when they enter view, inside sticky stages of 1.4-2.9 viewports; MacBook Pro runs 47 viewports and 22 sections with sticky spans of 2-4 viewports, each chapter opened by a short H2 ("Performance") and a one-line slogan ("Let there be delight.") ([Apple AirPods Pro](https://www.apple.com/airpods-pro/), [Apple MacBook Pro](https://www.apple.com/macbook-pro/)). The award-site honourees without WebGL do the same with GSAP: Nervana pins four one-viewport stages across a 15-viewport page, Essor Fitness chains six sticky spans of 1.2-3.2 viewports, Bevel Health moves from light app-screen chapters into a dark "Bevel Intelligence" chapter of stacked cards ([Nervana](https://nervana.reformcollective.com/), [Essor Fitness](https://coachsportifecublens.com/), [Bevel Health](https://www.bevel.health/)). What none of them do is a single pinned stage crossfading the same composition seven times; their chapters change layout, scale and palette between stages, and their progress signal is a sticky label or sub-nav rather than a bar (inference from the structure probes of those pages). NN/g's scrolljacking study found the majority of participants "at least mildly disoriented", one saying "if I were looking at this as a prospect, I would get severely agitated and just move on", and recommends minimising text inside scrolljacks and avoiding them on phones ([NN/g scrolljacking](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)). On phones the admired brands swap assets rather than remove chapters, and the responsive-scrollytelling rule is to stack static graphics when steps stand alone, never to override scrolling with steppers or swipes ([The Pudding](https://pudding.cool/process/responsive-scrollytelling/), [Eight Sleep](https://www.eightsleep.com/)).

The budget arithmetic favours stills and short clips over scrubbing. GSAP 3.13 made its whole toolset free for commercial use; core plus ScrollTrigger measured in TB Gym's own installed 3.15 package is **28.3 KB plus 18.0 KB gzipped**, SplitText 3.7 KB, Flip 9.7 KB ([GSAP 3.13](https://gsap.com/blog/3-13/), [GSAP licence](https://gsap.com/community/standard-license/); sizes measured locally). Media, not libraries, decides the score: Whoop's hero MP4 alone is **15.6 MB**, Tonal's 9.7-10 MB, Peloton's 360° canvas sequence 4.1 MB across 42 frames, a scroll-scrubbed all-intra video 8-13 MB, while Apple Fitness+'s loop is 1.17 MB for 5.2 s and Peloton's 1.76 MB for 9.3 s ([Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/), [Tonal](https://www.tonal.com/), [Peloton](https://www.onepeloton.com/), [Apple Fitness+](https://www.apple.com/apple-fitness-plus/), [hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/scroll-scrubbed-video-stutters-fix)). Lighthouse's phone simulation runs at 1.6 Mbps with a 4x CPU slowdown, so each megabyte costs about five seconds of the throttled budget ([Lighthouse throttling](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GoogleChrome/lighthouse/main/docs/throttling.md); the seconds-per-megabyte figure is an inference). Chrome excludes `opacity: 0` elements from LCP, so a hero fade-in waiting for JavaScript delays LCP by seconds; the pre-rendered HTML must paint the hero at full opacity ([Shopify](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/best-practices/performance/dont-hide-lcp-image-behind-animations)). Angular's `@defer` with `hydrate on viewport` lets the story chunk and GSAP load below the fold without changing the above-the-fold bundle ([Angular @defer](https://angular.dev/guide/templates/defer), [Angular incremental hydration](https://angular.dev/guide/incremental-hydration)). Lenis smooth scroll runs on 7 of 11 award pages inspected and on none of the seven brand homepages; Thoughtworks flags its accessibility issues and the scroll-hijack critique lists ten objections, so it stays out ([Lenis README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/darkroomengineering/lenis@1.3.26/README.md), [Thoughtworks Radar](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/languages-and-frameworks/lenis), [dontfuckwithscroll.com](https://dontfuckwithscroll.com/)). Native CSS scroll-driven animations now cover the cheap layers at 87.84 % support and run on the compositor in Safari 26.4 ([Can I use animation-timeline](https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_properties_animation-timeline), [WebKit 26.4](https://webkit.org/blog/17862/webkit-features-for-safari-26-4/)). One prerender trap is already on record: TrueCoach's stat counters render as "0K+ / 0K+ / 0M+" because their count-up never fires in a static render, so any number on TB Gym's pre-rendered page must be in the HTML at its final value ([TrueCoach](https://truecoach.co/)). Oura is the counter-example that keeps everyone honest: no video, no canvas, no GSAP, a 3.8 MB desktop transfer and 1.7 MB on the phone, and its agency reports a 42 % increase in daily sales and fifteen design awards after the redesign ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/), [Instrument](https://instrument.com/work/oura-smart-ring)).

## Two audiences, honest proof, and the shoot that makes both real

No category leader makes a visitor choose an audience in the hero. Routing lives in a "Business type" mega-menu (Mindbody, Trainerize with 13 types, Glofox with 9), in a late "who it's for" block, or in a rotating audience word inside one headline (Everfit's "FITNESS COACHES / GYMS & STUDIOS / NUTRITION COACHES / SPORT COACHES", Virtuagym's version) ([Mindbody](https://www.mindbodyonline.com/), [Trainerize](https://www.trainerize.com/), [Glofox](https://www.glofox.com/), [Everfit](https://everfit.io/), [Virtuagym](https://business.virtuagym.com/)). Stripe's split is the least generic: "Powering businesses of all sizes" as three stacked story blocks (enterprises, startups, platforms), each with its own headline, proof sentence, button and customer story, placed fourth of nine sections ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/)). NN/g's case against audience-based navigation is specific (people cannot tell which label applies, resent self-identifying, fear missing the other segment's offers), while its 2024 homepage principles accept calls to action "under clear headings addressing different user groups, such as For Job Seekers and For Businesses" below the elevator pitch ([NN/g audience navigation](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/audience-based-navigation/), [NN/g homepage principles](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/)). The two buying modes differ, and the hero can serve both without naming either: coach-first products lead with a self-serve trial and "no credit card" (TrueCoach, Hevy Coach, Trainerize, Everfit), every gym-first product leads with a demo (Mindbody, Glofox, PushPress, Zen Planner, even on pricing pages that print prices), and the generalists pair a self-serve primary with a sales secondary (Notion's "Get Notion free" + "Request a demo", Stripe's "Get started" + "Contact sales" in the nav) ([TrueCoach](https://truecoach.co/), [PushPress](https://www.pushpress.com/pricing), [Zen Planner](https://zenplanner.com/pricing/), [Notion](https://www.notion.com/), [Stripe](https://stripe.com/)). Gartner's March 2026 survey of 646 B2B buyers found **67 % prefer a rep-free experience** while still wanting seller input on fit questions, and ProductLed's guidance is to offer both because demos convert better and trials produce ten to twenty times the volume ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience), [ProductLed](https://productled.com/blog/demo-vs-trial)). The trust devices split the same way: coaches get self-serve reassurance in the hero; gyms get operational promises in plain lists (Mindbody's "What you get": unlimited users, live support, 1-on-1 setup, data migration), a switching path (Zen Planner's "Switch to Zen Planner" secondary hero button and three-step migration), and an FAQ that answers cost, comparison and switching ([Mindbody](https://www.mindbodyonline.com/), [Zen Planner](https://zenplanner.com/), [PushPress](https://www.pushpress.com/)). The category's pages run 8-13 desktop viewports, 9-12 sections and 1,000-2,500 words, with the coach sites near Unbounce's word band and the gym sites longer because of support and FAQ content (measurements of the sites above). On phones the leaders collapse to a text-first hero with one full-width button and the image after it; the instructive mistakes are Trainerize putting the image above the headline so the CTA lands at the bottom edge of the first viewport, and ABC Fitness stacking three bars so its CTA falls below it ([Trainerize](https://www.trainerize.com/), [ABC Fitness](https://abcfitness.com/)). The "cringe" the owner feels about a card pair is diagnosed by the sample: Mindbody, Zen Planner and Virtuagym use exactly that block and Trainerize the same three audiences as stacked rows, so the pattern is generic rather than wrong, and the five less generic devices are Stripe's story-led blocks with a real screen and a real decision per audience, PushPress's discipline chip row under the hero, a one-line range statement ("From inspiring coaches to training organizations"), a thin top strip that sends the non-buyer audience elsewhere ("Not a business? Book on the Mindbody app"), and a secondary hero button aimed at the second audience's real job ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/), [PushPress](https://www.pushpress.com/), [Everfit](https://everfit.io/), [Mindbody](https://www.mindbodyonline.com/), [Zen Planner](https://zenplanner.com/)). The research found no published A/B test of a single-audience versus two-audience hero, so the structure should be instrumented (hero CTA split, audience-section clicks) rather than assumed.

Proof before customers is a question of what is true now. Stanford's credibility guidelines (4,500+ participants) ask a site to make claims easy to verify, show a real organisation with a physical address, show "honest and trustworthy people" behind it, make contact easy, show recent updates and use restraint with promotion ([Stanford](https://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines/index.html)); NN/g's four factors are design quality, upfront disclosure, comprehensive and current content, and connection to the rest of the web, with the finding that participants "trusted testimonials from external sites more than those listed on the website itself" ([NN/g trustworthy design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)). Linear puts a dated changelog on its homepage before its customer quotes ([Linear](https://linear.app/)). The practitioner substitutes for a zero-customer page are a founder's note with a face and a track record, screenshots of genuine early interest, aggregate facts (years, reach), and events actually attended, with the warning that invented logos or quotes are "the fastest way to lose the trust you're trying to build" ([eChai](https://echai.ventures/startingup/website-landing/what-testimonials-or-logos-can-i-put-on-my-page-when-i-have-zero-customers-yet), [Harry Dry](https://marketingexamples.com/conversion/landing-page-guide)). The legal line is now explicit: the FTC's final rule bans testimonials "by someone who does not exist" or who lacks experience with the product and undisclosed insider testimonials, with civil penalties reported at about $51,744 per violation since 21 October 2024, and its Endorsement Guides require that an endorser's experience be real and typical or qualified ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials), [FTC Endorsement Guides](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)). Numbers can still appear, as product facts rather than adoption claims: Tonal's "one-pound increments", TrueCoach's explanation of how its 7/30/90-day compliance rate is computed, Eight Sleep's temperature range; TB Gym's equivalents are what a set row records (weight, reps, RPE 5-10 or RIR 0-5 in half steps, last time beside it), what a check-in stores, how a progress trend is computed ([Tonal](https://www.tonal.com/), [TrueCoach](https://truecoach.co/), [Eight Sleep](https://www.eightsleep.com/); the TB Gym examples are inferences from the product's own rules). Copying the competitors' proof strips would be both unprovable and uncredible, since three of them already claim "#1" at once ([Trainerize](https://www.trainerize.com/), [TrueCoach](https://truecoach.co/), [Everfit](https://everfit.io/)).

The owner's unease about the demo workspace resolves into a distinction the admired sites all make: sample data is demonstration, not proof, and nobody labels it. Linear's homepage screens show first names of its own team (Karri, Jori, Didier, Lena, Andreas) with fictional issue IDs; Apple's mocked-up messages use the real names of Apple employees and a new cast per release; Stripe's docs use "Jenny Rosen" at example.com; Trainerize, TrueCoach, Everfit and Strava show no person names in UI at all and reserve full names for real testimonials; no captured page carries a "demo" or "sample" caption ([Linear](https://linear.app/), [Max Read on Apple's fake texts](https://maxread.substack.com/p/a-literary-history-of-fake-texts), [Stripe API docs](https://docs.stripe.com/api/customers/create), [Trainerize](https://www.trainerize.com/)). Google's style guide says never to use real people's names in examples and to vary cultural identity; Microsoft's warns that fictitious names are "always potentially sensitive" and may need a coincidence disclaimer; right-of-publicity law turns on whether a real identity is evoked, and a disclaimer does not prevent a lawsuit ([Google style guide](https://developers.google.com/style/examples), [Thompson Hine](https://www.thompsonhine.com/insights/the-right-of-publicity/)). "Client A, Client B" fails the same guidance (unrealistic filler) and NN/g's finding that filler is ignored, so the owner's instinct is right; the convention that fits is first names only in UI, short and culturally mixed, a consistent cast across every screen, never a full name that could match a real coach or athlete in the Lebanese fitness scene, one small "Shown with sample data" caption per screenshot group, and no quote, rating or outcome ever attached to a sample person (inference from the sources above). The current demo cast carries full names (Lea Khoury, Maya Fakhoury, Rita Daher, Nour Hamdan) with common Lebanese surnames, and whether "Atlas Performance" collides with a real business was not checked; both are follow-ups before the names appear in a hero ([r62b-home-1440.png](C:\Users\Admin\Desktop\TB gym\docs\design\r62b-home-1440.png); the surname observation is an inference).

Photography is where the owner's budget buys the most, because the research says the admired pages' "cinematic" quality is made in the photograph and the CSS stays flat. Peloton's 2023 rebrand was shot "capturing the authenticity and rawness of real Peloton members in their actual homes" in a run-and-gun documentary style around the before, during and after of a workout; its 2025 Peloton IQ campaign used moving light rigs choreographed around athletes, probe lenses and macro close-ups in "a clean, architectural world"; Tonal's 2025 film is black-and-white; Future's "Here We Go" deliberately cast new mothers, injured athletes and older members rather than idealised bodies ([shots.net](https://shots.net/news/view/halal-enlists-arthur-couvat-for-pelotons-global-rebrand), [Stash](https://www.stashmedia.tv/?p=115804), [D&AD](https://www.dandad.org/work/d-ad-awards-archive/stop-working-out-in-the-past), [Ads of the World](https://www.adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/here-we-go-bccde451-6c28-4211-852c-eff78e161cd8)). ## Conclusion

The premium gap is a media gap, not a layout gap, and that reframes the owner's three complaints as one. The drawn phone, the boring stage and the unread cards all fail the same test the admired pages pass: is the thing on screen a real subject (a person, a current device, a bare window, a numeral) that the eye scrutinises, or a container that the eye skips? Every fix follows from that, and most are cheap: a free Apple bezel and a Playwright script, six alternating rows with real crops, dark bookends with a cream body, Plex at weight 500. The expensive one, the shoot, is also the one the owner can uniquely afford and the only one the competitors can copy least, since only 9.5 % of SaaS heroes use real people and the research places a coaching product with the service brands (Future, Peloton, Apple Fitness+) rather than the hardware brands.

Two of TB Gym's assets survive intact and become stronger under this reading. The "one week, both sides" story is still the thing no competitor shows, but it earns its keep as three or four short stages that change composition, not as one pinned crossfade; and the demo workspace remains the right source for every screen, provided it is treated as demonstration with first-name sample people and one honest caption, while proof moves into a layer that is true today (the founder, the product's facts, a dated changelog) and is designed to take real coaches' photos, clips and disclosed quotes after launch on the same release terms as the shoot. The one new discipline is restraint with the brand's own defaults: cream and italic serif have become everyone's shortcuts, so they must be used the way Oura uses them, with photography and a charcoal that is actually green, and lime must stay a signal rather than a fill, four units from Everfit's footer.

## Decision section 2: three directions, and a recommendation

All three directions share what the evidence fixes regardless of look: money terms and legal statements untouched (no price, offer or trial wording is proposed here); headline at 84 px / weight 500 / -3 % at 1440 and 46 px at 390, body 17-18 px, IBM Plex Mono eyebrows, upright Instrument Serif for numerals, italic accents limited to one word outside the hero; lime as text, numerals, eyebrow marks and one pill only; no purple, cyan, glow, grain or icon-tile cards; pills at 48 px for homepage CTAs only; device pictures from the Playwright-plus-bezel pipeline or bezel-less crops; features as real UI crops in the premium grammar; audiences routed by a nav route for gyms, a secondary hero action for the gym audience's real job, a thin strip for clients and a late story-led split with "For independent coaches" / "For gyms and coaching teams" headings; proof as a founder's note with a real portrait and place, a product-fact strip, a dated changelog line, the public demo marked as sample and a "Shown with sample data" caption, with real counts only from the database; first-name sample cast; hero painted at full opacity in the prerender; story chunk behind `@defer` with `hydrate on viewport`; reduced motion showing final states; no Lenis; pause controls on anything that moves longer than five seconds.

### Direction 1: Forest night, the product as the picture

**The look.** A near-black, green-cast page for the hero and the finale (#0a0f0d floor, #0f1513 page, #161e1b and #1d2724 surfaces, cream text, hairlines at 8-12 % white) around a cream editorial body, with the real app as the photographed subject: one current iPhone frame and bare coach windows with a 1 px edge, lit by backdrop rather than shadow. It is Linear's elevation method and Apple's section rhythm (112 px padding, 28 px media radius) in TB Gym's own hue, and it reads as an engineered tool rather than a fitness brand ([Linear](https://linear.app/), [Apple MacBook Pro](https://www.apple.com/macbook-pro/); palette contrasts computed from the existing tokens, all text pairs AAA except tertiary on cards).

**The hero.** Full-viewport dark stage; one iPhone 17 bezel over a 3x capture of the client's today screen at about 380 CSS px, whole and front-on, beside a 1:1 crop of the coach's Today window with a hairline border; a one- or two-line promise headline about the shared act of coaching (6-8 words), a 10-20-word sentence that names both audiences once, the primary pill "Start your coaching space" and a hairline secondary aimed at gyms ("See it run for a team" or a demo request, the owner's wording). No video; Oura and Future show a 49-64 KB still carries the weight ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/), [Future](https://www.future.co/)). The bezel route is the one grey area (a PWA inside Apple's frame); the fallback is the bezel-less fan, which avoids Apple's rules entirely ([Apple Marketing Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines/)).

**Section order.** Hero → factual strip (what it does today, built in Beirut, latest changelog date, link to the sample workspace) → the week as three or four chained stages of 2-3 viewports each with a different composition (phone alone; coach window alone; both with the number travelling; a glass card over dark), each opened by a typographic chapter title, with the Mon-Sun strip as the sticky chapter nav → six feature rows → the two-story audience split → "What you get" list (setup help, data import and export, support, data ownership, what happens when a coach leaves) → founder's note → FAQ (switching, who pays, data, team seats; no price figures proposed) → dark finale repeating both CTAs (inference assembled from [Apple MacBook Pro](https://www.apple.com/macbook-pro/), [Stripe](https://stripe.com/), [Mindbody](https://www.mindbodyonline.com/), [Linear](https://linear.app/), [Harry Dry](https://marketingexamples.com/conversion/landing-page-guide)).

**Features.** Alternating rows in the Hevy Coach and Tonal grammar: eyebrow with the plain feature name, a 3-7-word headline describing the moment, one 12-22-word sentence in the "it does X, so you can Y" shape, and a 1:1 real-UI crop on a tinted card whose key number is at least body-text size; each row pairs the coach's screen and the client's phone for the same moment, which no competitor does ([Hevy Coach](https://hevycoach.com/), [Tonal](https://www.tonal.com/); 1-2 developer days per the feature-section estimate).

**Audiences.** As shared above; in this direction each audience story uses a screen (the coach's program builder; the gym's team view with several coaches) rather than a photograph.

**Proof before launch.** The shared proof layer; the founder portrait is the only photograph on the page.

**Needs.** The device pipeline (Apple bezels free; Playwright already in the repo; about 12-16 hours, $0-20); no shoot; build about 6-8 working days (inference: 1-2 days for rows, 1.5-2 for the pipeline, the rest for the hero, stages, audience split, founder block, Vitest and Playwright coverage, two polish rounds); no shipped package (SplitText is inside the GSAP package already installed; `sharp` would be a dev-only build tool for compositing and can be avoided by compositing the bezel in an HTML page screenshotted by Playwright, an inference).

**Risks.** A dark hero without people risks the "dark hero that could belong to any product" tell if the accents ever drift to purple, cyan or glows; it delivers the Linear side of the owner's reference list more than the Whoop/Peloton side; Apple bezel compliance for a web app is unsettled; coaches may read an all-product first screen as cold (inference).

### Direction 2: Real coaches, real week

**The look.** A full-bleed photographic hero in low-key, single-source light (a coach reading a check-in on a phone at the rack, or building a week at a gym desk), fading to the green-cast night at the bottom, then a cream body and a dark finale, with the app shown two ways: UI cards lifted out of the photographs as glass cards (Eight Sleep's device) and one real iPhone frame with a screen recording for the client's side (Future's device). Warm skin, deep shadows, no neon; the CSS stays flat and the cinema is in the photograph ([Eight Sleep](https://www.eightsleep.com/), [Future](https://www.future.co/), [Peloton](https://www.onepeloton.com/)).

**The hero.** A still first (Future's hero is a 64 KB WebP; Oura's a 49 KB AVIF), preloaded as the LCP element, with the headline bottom-left or centred over a black fade; the same 6-8-word promise and 10-20-word sentence; primary "Start your coaching space", secondary for gyms; later, once measured, an 8-12 s muted loop at 0.9-2 MB with VP9 WebM before H.264 MP4, no audio track, `preload="none"`, a pause control, and a separate 9:16 edit or poster-only on phones ([Future](https://www.future.co/), [web.dev](https://web.dev/learn/performance/video-performance), [DebugBear](https://debugbear.com/blog/optimize-video-lcp), [WCAG 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)).

**Section order.** Hero (photo) → factual strip → the week as three or four stages, each a photograph of the moment with its real UI card lifted out or a device, alternating dark and cream, with chapter titles and the day strip as sticky nav → feature rows (as Direction 1, with the coach-and-client pairing) → the audience split, each half carrying one of the shoot's two subjects (an independent coach at a laptop; a gym floor with a team) and a screen → "What you get" → founder's note → FAQ → dark finale with the closing photograph (inference assembled from [Future](https://www.future.co/), [Eight Sleep](https://www.eightsleep.com/), [Stripe](https://stripe.com/)).

**Features.** Half the rows as photo-plus-lifted-card (the "during" moment Peloton favours: a client logging a set between sets with the set row floated beside her), half as frameless UI crops, so the section has pictures the eye scrutinises and numbers it can read ([NN/g photos](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/), [shots.net](https://shots.net/news/view/halal-enlists-arthur-couvat-for-pelotons-global-rebrand)).

**Audiences.** As shared, with the shoot giving each audience its own picture; the chip row of disciplines under the hero is optional if the owner wants to signal strength, physique and online coaching without role labels ([PushPress](https://www.pushpress.com/)).

**Proof before launch.** The shared proof layer, plus the rule the FTC makes necessary: the people in the shoot are photography, not customers, so they carry no quote, rating or "member" label unless they are genuinely coaches who used the product and consent, in which case any material connection (a founding discount, a free period) is disclosed beside the quote; after launch, each consenting coach supplies one sentence, a photo or the shoot portrait, and a 20-second phone clip on one release aligned with the shoot's terms ([FTC Endorsement Guides](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking), [DLA Piper on Lebanon's data law](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=LB)).

**Needs.** A one-day combined stills-and-video shoot in Beirut: two real coaches and one client with written releases (worldwide, web and social, perpetual, including composites and screen replacement, compensation stated), a gym as location with a property release, a documentary daylight register for most shots and one controlled low-key setup for the hero, devices shot dark with five markers for screen replacement or clamped for live screens, 30 retouched selects, 10-15 s loop-safe takes in 16:9 and 9:16 with no audio; TB Gym supplies 2x captures at the device's native ratio ([Logik forum](https://forum.logik.tv/t/screen-replacement-advice-for-producers/1773), [ASMP](https://www.asmp.org/strictly-business-blog/releases-for-professionals/)). No Lebanese rate card exists; the benchmarks are £500-1,000 a day for an established UK commercial photographer plus £10-25 per retouched image, US $1,000-3,000 a day, a 60-second brand video averaging $3,240, and Beirut described as among the region's most competitive, so three local quotes are the next step ([Whito](https://whito.co.uk/research/photography-costs-uk/), [Matt Hughes](https://www.matthughesphoto.com/d-photography/day-rate-for-photographer.html), [Advids](https://advids.co/pricing/how-much-brand-communication-video-creation-cost), [NEEDaFIXER](https://www.needafixer.com/filming-in-lebanon/)). Device pictures from the same pipeline; licensed, released stock (Stocksy $35-135 per image) or people-free environment shots only as placeholders ([Photutorial on Stocksy](https://photutorial.com/stocksy-review)). Build about 8-11 working days plus the shoot's lead time (inference: Direction 1's work plus the video delivery path, the photo fades and glass cards, and a second Lighthouse pass with and without the loop); no shipped package; ffmpeg installed locally for encoding (the machine currently has no ffprobe).

**Risks.** Cost and timing are the real ones; a shoot that slips leaves placeholders on the page. Unbounce's finding that video is neutral to negative means the loop must be justified by brand, measured for LCP and kept switchable to the still ([Unbounce video](https://unbounce.com/landing-page-optimization/video-on-landing-pages)). Photographs of non-customers must never be presented as endorsement. A brief that is not documentary produces the "stock fitness" look the owner is trying to leave. The phone budget (about 1 MB of chapter media, inference) must be enforced or the 90 score goes.

### Direction 3: Cream editorial with dark bookends

**The look.** The lowest-risk evolution of the current page: keep the cream body and the week story, but give the page Oura's restraint and Whoop's rhythm, a 900 px dark hero band and a 1,200-1,700 px dark finale, no video, a single serif numeral as the one-number device, fewer sections and almost no motion ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/), [Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/)).

**The hero.** A dark band with Oura's bezel-less fan of three to five real screens (the centre one largest, all legible) and the travelling number set upright in Instrument Serif at 88-120 px in lime; one headline, one sentence, one primary pill and a text link for gyms ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/)).

**Section order.** Hero → factual strip → the one-number chapter (the client's lift from a real screen, staged like Oura's "86 %" with a 1 px rule and a footnote that says it is sample data) → three week chapters as normal-flow sections, one asset each → feature tiles in Apple's "Get to know" form (a category eyebrow and a 3-6-word headline, a large crop filling 60-70 % of the tile, a two-size grid that stacks on phones rather than swiping) → the audience split → founder's note → FAQ → dark finale ([Apple iPhone](https://www.apple.com/iphone/), [SaaSFrame](https://www.saasframe.io/blog/designing-bento-grids-that-actually-work-a-2026-practical-guide); 2-3 developer days for the tile grid per the feature-section estimate).

**Features.** The picture-tile row; on phones a vertical stack, never a carousel ([NN/g horizontal scrolling](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/horizontal-scrolling/)).

**Audiences and proof.** As shared.

**Needs.** The device pipeline; no shoot; build about 4-6 working days (inference); no new package, and GSAP can be dropped from the homepage route altogether, as Oura ships none ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/)).

**Risks.** It is the direction most likely to look to the owner like "the same page again": still no people, still cream, and the serif and cream defaults need the most discipline here because they carry the page alone. It leaves the 9.5 % differentiator unused and will not reach the Whoop/Peloton feel the owner described (inference).

### Recommendation: Direction 2, staged on Direction 1's skeleton

Choose Direction 2 and sequence it so nothing waits on the shoot. The reasons are in the evidence rather than in taste. The "professional" impression the owner reports from Trainerize, TrueCoach and Everfit comes from a real human and a real device in the first screen at 50-64 % of the width, not from their layouts, which are ordinary ([Trainerize](https://www.trainerize.com/), [TrueCoach](https://truecoach.co/), [Everfit](https://everfit.io/)). The premium brands split by what they sell, and a coaching product sits with the service brands that lead with a person mid-effort ([Apple Fitness+](https://www.apple.com/apple-fitness-plus/), [Peloton](https://www.onepeloton.com/), [Future](https://www.future.co/)). Future proves the whole playbook works for a coaching app with no hardware at 7.6 MB and no animation library ([Future](https://www.future.co/)). Real people earn attention where generic people are filler, and only 9.5 % of SaaS heroes use them, so the shoot buys differentiation the competitors' corporate budgets have not bought ([NN/g photos](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/), [Tenet](https://www.wearetenet.com/blog/saas-hero-section-best-practices)). The owner's post-launch plan, photographs and clips of real coaches with consent, is the same system: the shoot's brief and releases become the testimonial template, and the glass-card and frame treatments take a real coach's screen without a redesign. And the two audiences are better served by two pictures than by two cards, which is the part of the current page the owner calls cringe.

Stage it in three steps so the page is never blocked. First, build Direction 1's skeleton now: the dark bookends, the device pipeline, the chained stages, the feature rows, the audience split and the proof layer, with the hero as the bezel-and-window composition; this is work Direction 2 needs anyway and it ships a page that already closes nine of the ten gaps. Second, brief and book the shoot in parallel from the shot list above, with the hero still as the one controlled setup and everything else documentary, and get three Beirut quotes against the benchmark bands. Third, when the selects arrive, swap the hero to the photograph, add the lifted cards to the week stages and the audience split, and only then measure the loop against the still on a phone before deciding whether it stays. Keep the three constraints that the research says decide the outcome: the hero still is the LCP element and renders in the prerender at full opacity; chapter media stays near 1 MB on phones; and nobody who appears in a photograph is presented as a customer until they are one and have said so in writing.
