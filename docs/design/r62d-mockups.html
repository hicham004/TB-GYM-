Five mockups in the final look, on real screens of the sample demo

Two heroes that differ only in their headline, one week stage, one feature row that pairs the coach's screen with the client's phone, and the audience split. Every picture is the app on "Oakline Strength", the first-name sample cast, photographed by the device pipeline: the client under Apple's iPhone 17 bezel with a drawn 9:41 status bar, the coach's screen as a 1:1 crop in a bare window. The grey block marks where the shoot's photograph goes.

Recommendation for R6.2f

Build hero B, the one promise line: "Coach both sides of the week." Six words, one idea, at 84 px in three short lines beside the two devices that prove it, with the devices keeping half the first screen, the ratio every admired hero measures at. The three beats (hero A) are a list of three ideas in the place where the premium pages put one; they are the week's story, so they live on as the chapter titles (Plan it. Lift it. See it.). A seven-word line ("Coach the whole week, from both sides.") needed four lines in this column and crowded the devices; six words is the limit at this size.

Changes to the row, seen while making these:

  1. The hero's coach window is the dark-mode Today at 1:1, so the lit phone is the one subject; the cream window beside the phone reads as two subjects fighting.
  2. A phone must be at least 330 CSS px wide to stay legible, so each feature row's coach crop is a 560 px region of the screen, not the whole window; the rows alternate which side the phone sits on.
  3. The week stage's chapter label is the Mon–Sun strip above the title, and the travelling number is an upright serif numeral in the copy column, never a lime chip.
  4. "Shown with sample data" is one mono caption per picture group; no person on a picture gets a quote, rating or outcome.
  5. The finale repeats the one lime pill; every other button on the page is forest or a hairline, so lime stays a signal.
